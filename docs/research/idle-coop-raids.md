# Co-op raids, difficulty curves and idle pacing in other games (research, 2026-10-15)

Why: Misa asked for inspiration from successful games and player sentiment on how to keep the weekly raid winnable, pace progression and reward the group. Researched by the researcher agent; Reddit was blocked, so sentiment comes from official forums, Steam reviews and press. Confidence is marked: confirmed (primary source read), secondary, unconfirmed.

## Weekly co-op bosses

- **Pooled boss, personal chest** (Raid: Shadow Legends Hydra, secondary): everyone's damage goes into one HP bar; each member's chest depends on their own damage; a full clear doubles chests. Per-person caps (3 keys a week, each champion once a week) stop one player carrying.
- **Equal rewards plus a participation floor** (Cookie Run: Kingdom guild battle, confirmed, cookierun.wiki): every member gets the same reward per cleared boss level; a per-member ticket cap and a guild battle cap; season rewards need 15 battles; a zero-damage attempt costs nothing; a practice mode.
- **Damage-tier chests** (AFK Arena Team Hunting, secondary).
- **Sentiment:** the Hydra is hated for random mechanics that punish teams "regardless of power" and for being "not auto friendly" (confirmed quotes, Plarium forum). Players dislike mechanics that ignore their build, not difficulty itself.

## Difficulty over weeks

- **Reset and climb, visible** (Cookie Run: Kingdom, confirmed): the boss resets each round and its level climbs during the fight, with the guild notified every 50 levels.
- **A pacing target** (Final Fantasy XIV, confirmed, Yoshida on tomestones): the weekly cap was set around the expected clear pace; raising it would grow the group of players who feel "obligated to cap", so options were added instead.
- **Catch-up** is common in gacha games: content tuned so a "reasonably built" account clears it; beginner banners and stockpiles (secondary).
- **Sentiment:** a tough boss is accepted when the cap is predictable; mandatory weekly chores are resented.

## Idle pacing

- **Clocks that lengthen** (Eric Guan, idle game design principles, confirmed): long early sessions, then hourly, daily, weekly check-ins; costs grow faster than output (x1.15 against x1.1); several currencies on purpose, so frequent and infrequent players each have something.
- **Reveal gradually**: show the next 2 or 3 upgrades, not all of them; prestige must feel like acceleration (secondary).
- **Sentiment** (Idle Champions, confirmed quotes): a 23,000-hour player quit when new systems turned "idle" into "24x7 babysitting"; a hard wall forced grinding; players asked to skip content they had outgrown.

## Group-level gating

- **Genshin Impact World Level** raises enemy level and drop quality together, gated by quests; players asked for an opt-down, added in 1.4 (lower by one, once a day, worse drops), and later an optional harder tier (secondary). It is per player, not per group. No sources on server-age or guild-level gating of solo content were found: treat it as a hypothesis to test.

## Loot that changes play

- **Targeted source, random roll** (Summoners War runes: choose the dungeon for the set, the roll is random; Destiny 2 Deepsight and patterns target a weapon for a separate currency; secondary).
- **WoW Great Vault** (confirmed, BlizzardWatch and Blizzard forums): a weekly pick from tiered activities. Praised as a path to good gear for players outside the hardest raids; criticised for two layers of randomness ("Box of Disappointment") and for making ordinary drops feel worthless.
- **Diablo 4**: affix overload, players asked for a loot filter (secondary).
- **Sentiment:** choosing a target is liked; double randomness is hated.

## What this suggests for Leet Creatures

1. A **guaranteed reward for every participant** of a won raid, not a lottery (built 2026-10-15: one gear piece or a new blueprint, better at higher Boss Rank).
2. **Show the group's progress** (the boss's HP bar across the week, the Boss Rank), not individual rankings, so nobody feels carried.
3. **Bosses that punish setups, not builds:** rank-gated moves should reward smart placement and protection, never strip what players built at random (the Hydra lesson).
4. **Low-attention idle loops:** avoid "must check now" mechanics; the live finale is the one appointment a week.
5. **An opt-down after losses** is worth considering: a struggling server could fight one rank lower for smaller rewards.
6. **Server-rank gating** of solo content is unproven; measure first whether one player's dungeon gear actually skews raids.
7. **Targeted gear:** each source (expedition zone, dungeon depth, raid boss) rolls from its own pool, so players choose what to chase; one layer of randomness only.
