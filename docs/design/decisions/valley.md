## 2026-11-06: Patchwork pieces built: the plainest piece of the shard's element, Rare at best

ID: `2026-11-06-patchwork-pieces-built-the-plainest-piece` · Status: current · Amends: `2026-11-06-patchwork-pieces-blueprints-that-take-any`

**Decision.** Patchwork pieces are built as three blueprints in `packages/game/src/content/data/patchwork.json` (Patchwork Cap, Shawl and Charm, 72, 72 and 90 scrap with 4, 4 and 5 shards), not as gear bases. A piece is the plainest base of its slot in the shard's element (a Kelp Cap for Tide), so the art, tint, element badge and wearing rules are those of any gear. "Uncommon" is the game's Rare: a patchwork piece is Common or Rare (an Epic draw becomes Rare; no Reach), and rolls fewer lines (Common none, Rare one, against one and two). The element is the order's, or "most held": the pile biggest when the piece starts, a tie going to the first element. "For my team" keeps making proper pieces; the player gives a waiting anvil a patchwork order. The name stays "Patchwork" (the Patch Scarf is the old neutral piece, so the scarf is a Shawl).

**Reasoning.** A base per element would be fifteen new gear bases with no new look, and the item would not be an ordinary element piece; resolving to the existing plain base keeps one code path for wearing, art and lines. Rolling fewer lines and capping at Rare is what keeps them from replacing proper blueprints. The prices came from the economy lab: its player orders them only when an anvil would tinker, from spare scrap and shards (the whole team's dressing and the exchange's reserve first), and with these prices T-7 and T-7b pass on `mixed8-market` where they failed, with T-3, T-4 and T-4b unharmed. A team order is not widened because "for my team" is about dressing weak slots with the best base the fire allows; a patchwork piece is by design worse.

## 2026-11-06: Patchwork pieces: blueprints that take any shard, with scrap [Misa]

ID: `2026-11-06-patchwork-pieces-blueprints-that-take-any` · Status: current, amended by `2026-11-06-patchwork-pieces-built-the-plainest-piece`

**Decision.** A few blueprints, one per slot (Hat, Scarf, Charm), known to every player from the start, take shards of any element plus scrap. The piece's element is the shard's; the anvil uses the element the player holds most of unless the order names one. They roll Common or Uncommon only, with fewer good lines than proper blueprints, so they never replace them. Their costs show as icons (decision `2026-11-06-costs-show-the-resource-s-icon`). "Patchwork" is a working name.

**Reasoning.** Misa (2026-11-06): "offering more stuff to craft that actually uses resources that are available ... items that can be crafted with any shard, and ... the game automatically picks the shard that they have most of, unless otherwise specified." A waiting anvil gets real work instead of tinkering, a lopsided shard pile has a use, and scrap gets a second sink, which the lab needs: with spare shards going to the Market, T-7/T-7b (the scrap pile) failed on the shipped design.

## 2026-11-06: The Market takes shards of any element, with no cap **[Misa]**

ID: `2026-11-06-the-market-takes-shards-of-any` · Status: current · Amends: `2026-11-06-the-market-is-the-second-guided`

**Decision.** Misa (2026-11-06): "the resources waiting in the forge as extra resources could be channeled into the market ... allow players to just put their resources in and get the market open ... it's a pain point for our players to have some resources piling up while others are waiting for them." The Market's project takes **shards of any element, with no cap** (they may fill the whole project), alongside Acorns and scrap, at **one value for every shard**: `VALLEY_RULES.commons.oneShot.market.shardValue` = 0.25 of an Acorn's worth (4 shards count as 1 Acorn). Built as "shards stand in for any part": the Market's shard part is as big as the project (`size / shardValue`), progress is the parts' value together never past the size, the project is done when that reaches the size, and a part's room never passes what the project still lacks (`anyShardValue`, `partNeeds`, `projectProgress`, `projectDone`, `partRoom`). The gift route counts every shard in full there (never the exchange's 3 for 1) and takes any element, still a keyed spend through `runKeyed`; every other place is unchanged (its own element in full, others 3 for 1). The view's project gains `anyShard` (the value, or null). Client: the board says "Shards of any kind count too, as many as you like: 4 shards count as 1 Acorn"; the Give drawer lists a row for each element the player holds, the biggest pile first (so that is the one suggested; they can pick another). The Market's size (0.25) and split (half Acorns, half scrap) are unchanged. Lab policy: a player gives, at each visit while the Market is open, every shard above what the Forge's next orders need of that element (never under the exchange's reserve of 8), the biggest pile first.

**Reasoning.** Measured on `mixed8-market` (10 seeds, same draws): the Market is built on day 12.8 to 14.3 (median 13.8) without the rule and on day 10.8 to 12.8 (median 11.8) with 0.25; week 2's shard stall 74% falls to 65%; T-4b 10% falls to 9% (20 seeds: 9% to 8%); T-3a (6.6 days) and T-3c (6 level-ups) do not move. Values swept (median build day, 10 seeds): 0.1 gives 13.3, 0.2 gives 12.3, 0.25 gives 11.8, 0.5 gives 10.8, 1 gives 9.8, 2 gives 8.8. Misa liked the opening about day 13 to 15 with the Forge waiting in week 2, so the relief is kept modest: 0.25 opens it about two days sooner and keeps most of the waiting week, and the value stays under a scrap's (0.5) because the lab's players give their whole surplus; 0.5 or more would open it on day 10 or sooner. **A known miss:** T-7 and T-7b on this study variant (a daily player's scrap pile, the Market's scrap part having been a sink) fail at 10 seeds with the rule on (T-7 passes at 20 seeds, T-7b fails), and that variant was already a knife edge: with no shard rule, a Market of 0.24, 0.26 or 0.28 of the base instead of 0.25 fails both at 10 seeds. The cause is real as well as noisy: a shard given is a piece the Forge does not make later (a piece costs 36 scrap and 1 shard), so the scrap pile grows, and shards of the Market's own giving displace some of its scrap part. No target was loosened; the judged targets run on the day-one-broker `mixed8` server (no Market) and do not move.

## 2026-11-06: The Market is the second guided project, straight after the hearth (Q48, built)

ID: `2026-11-06-the-market-is-the-second-guided` · Status: current, amended by `2026-11-06-the-market-takes-shards-of-any` · Amends: `2026-11-06-phase-5-step-6-the-commons`

**Decision.** Builds Q48 (answer A, round 19), which steps 6 and 7 had not: the guided order is **the hearth, then the Market alone, then the Forge, then every other place** (`VALLEY_RULES.commons.guided`, V-COM-15). `mayOpenProject` opens a guided project once the ones before it have levelled (the Market counts as levelled when built, level 2); a place not named opens once all three have. This also amends `2026-11-06-phase-5-step-7-the-market`, which made the Market the last place to open (with the rest, after the hearth and the Forge). The Market's project is small: `oneShot.market.sizeFactor` 0.25 of the base size (was "sized as a level-2 project", 1.0; the hearth's first level is 0.0875), still paid half Acorns, half scrap, no shards (`oneShot.sizeLevel` became `sizeFactor`, a share of the base, so the number is the rule's own). The lab follows the same rule: `openMarket` runs in `refreshProjects` when the hearth has levelled (the stall-share trigger `MARKET_OPENS_AT` is gone), and a variant with no Market (the day-one broker) skips it in the order. Client: a waiting place says what it waits for ("Opens once the hearth has grown" for the Market, "once the Market is built" for the Forge).

**Reasoning.** With the order of step 7 the Market was built in week 12 (median) on `mixed8-market`; once Q64 locked the exchange until the Market stood, the Forge's shard stall over weeks 2 to 12 rose from 43% (day-one tap) to 73% (T-4b's 25% line). Q48 asked for the fix to arrive as the first anvils start waiting. Measured on `mixed8-market` (10 seeds): size 0.25 builds the Market on day 12.8 to 14.8 across seeds (opened day 6 to 8, after the hearth levels), T-4b 5% (median over 10 seeds; 9% over 20; 11% in the report's 6), T-3a unchanged at day 6.6. A smaller Market (0.0875 to 0.2) is built by day 10 to 12 but leaves the scrap pile flagged dead (T-7b) at 10 seeds, because the old 4,500 scrap of a level-2 Market was a real scrap sink and the Forge's own project opens later; 0.25 and 0.35 pass T-7b at 10 seeds, and 0.6 and up fail T-4b. T-7 and T-7b on this variant still flip with the seed count (they fail at 6 seeds, pass at 10 and 20): the variant is a study, the judged targets run on the day-one `mixed8` server and did not move. Week 2 itself still stalls (67%), because the Market is built in days 12 to 15.

## 2026-11-06: Q64 built: the exchange at the Market only, the opening as a paper headline, the lab without an early exchange

ID: `2026-11-06-q64-built-the-exchange-at-the` · Status: current

**Decision.** Builds `2026-11-06-the-shard-exchange-opens-with-the` behind `VALLEY_SYSTEMS`. (1) `POST /api/valley/exchange` checks the Market's level (one `valley_places` row) after the settle and before the keyed spend and answers 409 `market_not_built`; nothing is stored under the key, so it is free once built. The view's `market.open` is the flag the client reads. (2) The opening is a paper headline `market-open` (group `server`, `HIGHLIGHT_WEIGHT.marketOpen` 120, above every other story, so it leads). The paper log gains `market` (optional, default false); migration 63 adds `valley_gazette.market_told`. On a view with the Market built, the one gazette row the view reads anyway is checked: not told yet means the story joins the unread paper (or makes a paper of its own, `awayHours` 0) and `market_told` is set, so it is told once per player, replays until the paper is read, and reaches players who were offline when it opened (they get it on their next look, wherever they are in the three-hour rule: `publish` still needs 3 hours away for the rest of the paper, the Market story does not). A player who first joins after the Market is built is told too (no per-player "joined" stamp exists to tell them apart). (3) Client: the exchange `Exchange` drawer moved to `valley/Exchange.tsx` and opens from a row on the Market's page; the Forge's shelf lost its Trade button; `tinkerLine` takes whether the Market is built; the paper's Market scene needed a door inside the stall in `SceneCrop`'s table. (4) Lab: `exchangeOn(server)`: in the `market` variants nobody exchanges before the Market is built; the day-one variants keep it. The judged targets run on the day-one `mixed8` server, so none moved; the shipped-design (`mixed8-market`) shard stall over weeks 2 to 12 went from 43% to 73% (T-4b's 25% line fails there, it already did at 43%), and its scrap pile is flagged dead (T-7, T-7b) because anvils tinker instead of spending scrap on pieces.

**Reasoning.** The check sits before the keyed spend so a refusal never writes a key row. The told mark has to outlive a read paper, so it is a column on the row (not in the log, which is replaced). Putting the story in the paper log, not a second table, reuses the replay, the join and the close. The lab's `mixed8` is the judged server and models the broker as always there; the Market variants are the shipped design, so the lab change is where the shipped behaviour shows. The lead and Misa should see that the lab's Market takes about 9 to 12 weeks to build and the stall in that time is about two thirds of anvil time: the "first week or two" the design assumed (V-FORGE-11, Q48) is not what the lab shows.

## 2026-11-06: Phase 5 step 7: the Market and the broker (a one-shot place, the broker seat, trades inside the pass, the Market's page)

ID: `2026-11-06-phase-5-step-7-the-market` · Status: current

**Decision.** Migration 62 (one column: `valley_creatures.trade_hours`), behind `VALLEY_SYSTEMS`. Server and client together. Seven rules:

- **The Market is a place built once (V-FORGE-11, V-COM-15).** `market` is the last of `commons.places`, so it opens with the rest once the hearth and the Forge have levelled (the lead's brief: after the Forge's, per 6a; V-COM-15 read alone would open it with the Forge's). `commons.oneShot.market` (new rules data) says three things: its project is sized as a level-2 project (the lab's `openMarket`), it is paid in Acorns and scrap only (split 0.5 / 0.5, no shards: the lab's "market" variant and the doc, "paid in Acorns and scrap"), and it never opens a second project. The pure helpers take the place (`partNeeds`, `projectProgress`, `projectDone`, `partRoom`, `valueToUnits`, `projectSize`, `isOneShot`), `mayOpenProject` is false for a built one, and the commons view gains the state `done` (built; level 2). A gift of shards to it is a 409 `part_full`. `ValleyCommons.load` makes the row of a place added after the valley opened (a server that opened its valley before step 7 gets the Market's row on its next read). The Market's level-up warms the fire like any place (the lab does).
- **The broker is a seat, not a store (V-FORGE-11).** A `valley_seats` row `place = 'market'`, `kind = 'broker'`, one per player, made empty, free, the first time the player looks after the Market is built (`ValleySettle.seats`, one insert; the seat id then exists for the existing `POST /api/valley/seat`, which posts, swaps and empties it as at any seat; kit and destination answer 400). `destination_id` is what it trades toward (`any` or an element) and `shard_carry` the change owed (see below). The docs price no broker seat, and the lab gives `broker.seats = 1`, so there is no buy route.
- **Trades happen inside the lazy pass, as part of the anvils' start.** The lab trades hour by hour whenever an order is short of a shard, never storing anything; so the pure `forgeInstants` runs the broker after the anvils at each instant: it tops each element the orders will need (the next three pieces' shards of each anvil's current order, the team's weak slots as the lab, and the `toward` element's three) from the biggest spare pile (above `forge.giveReserveShards`), at the rate and crit chance of its creature's trading mastery (`broker.tiers`, the mastery steps of V-POST-12), then the anvils that waited try again, until nothing more trades: a fixed point, so settling again at the same moment, or after any split, changes nothing (tested for splits and for the same `now`). A rate such as 2.4 on whole shards pays the next whole number and keeps the change in `shard_carry` (the broker's credit, 0 to 1), so the average is the rate. Crits are live draws of the pass's injected rng (`crypto` in production). Its trading hours (`trade_hours`) grow with the effective hours while a creature stands there (not away in the raid, not before it joined) whether or not anything trades, and the pass splits where the step changes the terms. **No store, so nothing "comes home":** what it trades is on the shelf as it happens, like tinkering scrap. It needs the Forge's state to trade (a player with no anvil has nothing to trade for, except through `toward`, which needs the shelf too); mastery grows regardless.
- **Routes.** Posting reuses `POST /api/valley/seat`; `POST /api/valley/broker { seatId, toward: element | null }` sets what it trades toward (strict body, 404 with the flag off before the body is read, free, 400 for a seat that is not a broker's, 404 for another player's). The settle runs before and after. The valley view gains `market { open, seat | null }` (the seat: creature, `paused`, `toward`, mastery, the terms now `{ rate, critBp, critYield }`, the next step's terms and the hours it comes at, and `blocked`: the element an anvil still waits for although the broker works, because no spare pile can pay). The creature state `trading` joins `resting | scouting | smithing | helping`.
- **No headline kind in the paper.** The Gazette's ranking has kinds for finds, pieces, mastery and the valley; trades are bookkeeping that the doc keeps out of the return walk the way it keeps tinkering out (V-FORGE-2), and a "traded N shards" story would be the most frequent and least interesting line in every paper. The broker's mastery step does reach the paper's mastery headline only if `rec.mastery` is given a kind for it, which it is not (a later call).
- **Client.** The Market's "not yet open" scene stays exactly as Misa saw it until the project is open (`marketState`: waiting). Then the Market is a band scene (`PlaceScene`, `CAST_PLACES += market`: the stall, scaffolding as the project fills, the broker and the helper standing at it) over `MarketPage`: while it is built, the one `CommonsBoard` of every place (no shards offered); once built, one seat card of the Signpost's family (`sp-seat`, `ui/Slot` row: the broker, and "Trades toward"; one line "Trades 12 for 5, 10% bonus" or "Waiting for spare shards to trade for Tide", which opens its rates in a `ui/Drawer` with a `DataTable`; the creature in the shared `RosterPicker`, "toward" in a `Drawer` + `ChoiceList`; a hand carries a creature from the `Tray` onto it). The tray says "Trading" (a gem mark, sorted after the smiths); the Places line says "being built", "a free broker seat", "the broker trades" or "the broker waits for spare shards"; the Market joins the opened places once its project is under way. No new bubble (the Forge's long shortage bubble already says the thing the broker is for).
- **Pricing and the lab.** `read-budget.test.ts` "the broker": an arrival with a broker that trades 971 reads and 118 written (the Forge's arrival with two anvils is 939 and 110), a look 836 and 67, the first look after the Market is built (the seat made) 833 and 61, setting what it trades toward 1,007 and 63; `query-plan-audit.test.ts` adds the broker's statements; `read-day.test.ts` adds a flag-on day with a broker for every player, trading for two anvils each: 134,621 rows read (134,016 without the Market) and 5,174 written (5,098), inside the budgets. Lab: `valley-market-parity.test.ts` runs `mixed8-market` and `mixed8-nobroker` seeded 1 to 3 over 14 weeks: the Market is built in week 1 or 2, and after it the server's shard stall is about 0.04 against 0.27 without a broker (the test holds it under half); on the pass, one player's two anvils with a shelf rich in two other elements wait 100% of the time without a broker and 0% with one, against the lab's 8% of the no-broker waiting left (the pass removes at least as much as the lab; it has the shelf and no supply shortfall, the lab's shards run out).

**Reasoning.** The broker's whole job in the lab is to keep the shelf stocked, so it belongs where the shelf is read, in the pass, not in a store of its own that would have to be paid out and a second place to write; the fixed-point rule is what keeps the lazy pass split-invariant with a live-random trade in it. A one-shot place with its own split and size is data (`commons.oneShot`) and three optional arguments, so the commons stays one machine for every place. The Market's page reuses every part (band scene, seat card, picker, drawer, board) and adds one CSS rule (a line of words that wraps).

**Not built / left out.** A bonus line in the paper (above); Market levels beyond the first (the doc's "later Market levels improve the rate" is a later idea); the School as a second buyer of the broker's shards (the School is step 9, `weakByElement` is the Forge's only); the broker's mastery perk at another post; the lab's opening condition (the Market opens when anvils wait 15% of a week for shards: the server opens it in the guided order, a server-wide stat would cost a read on every pass).

**Reasoning.**

## 2026-11-06: Phase 5 step 6b: the commons on the client (one project board for every place, the give drawer, scaffolding, helpers, plaque, collections, the fire, the grand bonfire, keepsakes)

ID: `2026-11-06-phase-5-step-6b-the-commons` · Status: current

**Decision.** The client half of step 6, behind `VALLEY_SYSTEMS` (it reads the optional `valley.commons` block; absent, nothing draws and the pages are as before). No server change. Seven rules:

- **One board for every place (V-COM-6).** `valley/CommonsBoard` is the only component of a place's project: a card of the seat family (`.sp-seat`, `ui/Bar`, `ui/Slot`, `ui/ActionButton`, `ui/Tag`) with the level ("Level 2 / 5"), the open project's bar and what is still needed ("Still needed: 260 Acorns, 519 scrap, 65 Grove shards"), the player's own part ("You gave 31% of this.", never anyone else's amount), one gold Give button, a row for the collection and a row for the plaque. A place that builds says "The hearth is being built, 2 days left"; a place the guided start has not reached says when it opens. It sits in the page of the Signpost and the Forge (under the seat cards, in their drag context), in the Paddock's page above the creatures, and for the Bonfire (a full-screen scene with no page) it opens in one `ui/Drawer` from the scene's pinned gold button, where Give, the plaque, the collection and the helper picker are drill-ins of that one drawer (`ui/DrillHead`), never a second layer. The School and the Market have no board: their places are still "not open yet" (steps 7 and 9).
- **Giving (V-COM-1, 5).** Give opens a list of the parts the player holds (Acorns, scrap, the place's own shards, and shards of another element only when they have the exchange's rate in hand, "3 for 1"), each with why it cannot be given; a part opens the amount (`ui/CountStepper` with an "All", starting at what the project still takes) and one `SpendConfirm` with the price. The gift is a keyed spend: `ActionIds.gift(place, part, units, room)` through `runKeyed`, `POST /api/valley/gift`; a retry reuses its key. A gift that raises the level says so in the notice line.
- **The scene shows the work (V-COM-6).** `valley/scaffold.ts` draws new-timber scaffolding (poles, boards and braces, pale wood with an ink edge so it stands out from the building's own wood) as a sprite the size of the place's art, up to a twelfth of its height for each step of the project's fill (never the last step: the level is raised then). It is drawn over the art in the band scenes of the Signpost, the Forge and the Paddock and in the Bonfire's scene. A creature on a communal seat stands in the scene: with the scouts and smiths at the Signpost and the Forge, at the rails at the Paddock (it no longer wanders there), as the lamplighter beside the fire at the Bonfire. In the tray it is "Helping" (a small hand mark; the new tray state sorts after "Smithing").
- **Communal seats (V-COM-14).** The seat is the shared slot row on the board (one per player per place), posted with `ui/RosterPicker` from a page and by a drag from the tray onto the board (`data-seat="communal:<place>"`, handled in the Signpost's and the Forge's drag context), from the creature list drill-in inside the Bonfire's drawer. A creature at another post swaps, as at every seat.
- **Plaque, keepsake, collections (V-COM-2, 3, 12).** The plaque opens from its row in a drawer as the names the server sent (alphabetical, no amounts, none of the numbers); the player's keepsakes stand on a shelf in the House (`valley/Keepsakes`, one small badge per place they helped build a level of). A collection's five sockets show their source and where the find turns up (from the source id), and a set one the finder's name; each place's board has its name from the design (The First Fires, Stones of the Roads, Embers of the First Fire, Old Friends, The Lost Lessons).
- **The fire and the hearth (V-COM-13, V-BON-7).** The Forge's page opens with the fire: `valley/CommonsFire`, the valley's small flame sprite at a pixel scale that steps up at each item-level threshold (10, 25, 50, 80), "Pieces come out at level 12." and "At level 25: a stronger modifier tier.", and its numbers and the last five things that warmed it in a drawer. The Bonfire's art follows its level with the two arts the library has: the plain fire at level 1, the grand hearth (taller fire, paved ring, log benches, lanterns) from level 2 (`GRAND_LEVEL`), so levels 3 to 5 reuse the grand art (no art of their own yet: a request for the art library); the map draws it too. The hearth's bonus is the calm line under the scene ("Hearth adds 8% to every creature's growth.") and not a number anywhere else.
- **Places list and bubbles.** A place's line mentions its project only while it builds ("the Forge is being built, 2 days left"; the Bonfire's line, its resting count, gives way to "the hearth is being built, 2 days left"); an open project adds nothing. **No new bubbles:** an open project is permanent news (every server has one open at every place until the player has given to each), so a bubble would sit on three or four places for good and make the map noisy; the board and the pinned Bonfire button say it where the player is.

**Reasoning.** Misa asked for clean, uncluttered screens: one board component carries every place's project, so a place differs only by data, and everything it opens is a drawer or a drill-in of one that already exists in the Signpost's and the Forge's family (`ux-patterns.md`, "Shared components"). Scaffolding is a sprite like the places' own art, so it is pixel-exact at the scene's scale and costs nothing to draw per frame (cached by art and step). Helpers standing in the scene follow V-COM-6 ("creatures on communal seats are seen working") and the creature-by-its-feet depth rules (`Stander`, `depthOf`). The Bonfire could not take a page card without becoming a band scene, a visible change to a design Misa has seen, so it keeps its full-screen scene and gains one gold button plus one drawer. The collection's find names come from the source ids the server sends, so a new kind of find shows its source with no server change. Tested: `commons-view.test.ts` (words, the give rows and their limits), `CommonsBoard.test.tsx` (every state, give, the plaque with no numbers, the collection, the helper picker, the sheet as one drawer), `commons-scene.test.ts` (scaffolding steps, helpers in the scenes, the tray, the Places line, no new bubble), `scaffold.test.ts`, the API tests, and the e2e `valley systems on: ...` steps at 390, 1100 and 1440 px with `expectTopmost` on every board, drawer and the plates at the fire.

## 2026-10-30: Valley: a bag of 200 with real tools, a rising item level cap to try, no pity floor (round 10)

ID: `2026-10-30-valley-a-bag-of-200-with` · Status: current

Misa's answers to the valley plan's Q24 to Q26 (`docs/design/valley/questions.md`; `docs/design/valley/systems/gear.md` V-GEAR-6 to V-GEAR-9).

- **The bag (Q24).** Worn items do not count; the bag holds 200. Players get filter, sort and search (rarity, item level, slot, element, stats, modifier lines), bulk dismantling, stats and comparison at a glance instead of a drawer per item, a creature-side flow to find a creature's best item, and a visual highlight for items that are really good (a high item level common can beat a low item level epic). No loot filter rules: "you're kind of expecting to see this big pile of loot".
- **Item level (Q25).** The source sets it under a server cap that rises with raid wins and slowly with time; tried and watched, since Misa doubts players will welcome their best craft being outdated, and he prefers new crafts over upgrading one item forever.

This restores blueprint marks, which the scouts and Forge panel had removed.

## 2026-10-29: Valley: one shared valley, posts instead of timers, away growth in steps (round 1)

ID: `2026-10-29-valley-one-shared-valley-posts-instead` · Status: current

Misa's answers to the valley plan's round 1 (`docs/design/valley/questions.md`, Q1 to Q3).

- **One valley for the whole server (Q1).** Everyone's creatures live and work in the same places; the places belong to the server; each player's house is their own space. Personal and shared progress take turns being the limit: "A player might have all the resources to unlock a third anvil, but the forge may not be able to fit three anvils per player yet. - **Posts replace every timed task (Q2).** Expeditions' timed trips and the Workshop's timed jobs are scrapped; scouts and smiths work continuously. One plain scalar on every idle gain from the time since the last visit (V-POST-10; the steps are set in the economy lab). Full stores and finished queues bring players back on their own; the scalar does not try to. Today's lounge cap of 60 XP a creature a day goes with it. Goal G-3 and filter F-1 now say absence never takes away what a player has, rather than that it costs nothing.

## 2026-10-29: Valley: be brave, and decide in small rounds of questions

ID: `2026-10-29-valley-be-brave-and-decide-in` · Status: current

Misa: "I am more inclined to scrap existing features, systems and design choices than cling on to them. I want to be brave. Any existing players and their achievements can be remembered by an "I was there" type of memento if nothing else." And: "ask them 1-3 at a time and always numbered... I need you to challenge my principles and answers, even to questions I've previously answered, if they contradict with my own output, research or demonstrable facts."

- **Be brave (goal G-0 in `docs/design/valley/goals.md`).** Nothing in today's game is kept because it exists. A scrapped system leaves its players an "I was there" memento, decided at the migration (valley roadmap phase 6); old systems are not kept alive to protect progress. This extends the 2026-10-28 rule that content changes are not held back by what players own.
- **Question rounds.** The valley plan moves forward in rounds of one to three numbered questions (numbers run on across rounds), each with why it matters, options with examples and the lead's pick (`docs/design/valley/deciding.md`). Every question and answer is kept in the ledger `docs/design/valley/questions.md`, which replaces `open-questions.md`; the `valley-round` skill runs a round.
- **Challenges.** The lead checks every answer, old or new, against Misa's goals, earlier answers, the research and the numbers, and raises a conflict once, as a numbered question.
