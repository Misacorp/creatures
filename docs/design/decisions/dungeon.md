## 2026-10-22: Foe move lines say what a move hits; "Charged:" replaces "announced a round ahead"

ID: `2026-10-22-foe-move-lines-say-what-a` · Status: current

- **Wording** (`gimmickShort`, the pre-fight drawer, the stage's room banner and the raid's icon labels). A line says what the move does, then how often, with no comma: "Slams your strongest creature and the ones beside it every 4 rounds". A charged move starts with "Charged:" and then says what it hits ("Charged: slams your strongest creature and the ones beside it every 4 rounds"); that it is announced a round ahead is what "charged" means (Misa: implied by "charged", which may later become a keyword players can act on, for example to interrupt). A slam names its aim (strongest, best Payoff, random, left); on the dungeon's row the left creature has one neighbour, so "the one beside it".
- **Layout.** Each drawer move row is a bullet: the glyph in its own column, centred on the first line of text (`height: 1lh`), the pixel-font name then the description inline, wrapped lines under the name.
- **Fonts stay two.** Misa asked whether the description could use the pixel font. Jersey 10 has one weight, so the name and its description would look the same; Misa's rule was to keep the two fonts (pixel name, body-font description) in that case.

## 2026-10-22: Molten Shell is a frequent partial Backlash, not a rare full reflect (replaces the reflective shield above)

ID: `2026-10-22-molten-shell-is-a-frequent-partial` · Status: current

Misa found a 5% full reflect too swingy: a lucky roll ruins a good run, and most runs never see it. The `reflect_chance` op is replaced by `backlash` (`chanceBp`, `shareBp`; the old op was never in a release, so no stored run holds it, and the raid's `reflect_first_reaction` stays so stored runs with the old Thorns reflect still replay).

- **What it does.** Each reaction a living creature sets off has `chanceBp` chance (5000: half the time) to backfire. The foe still takes the whole reaction (plain and bonus), setup is credited and the mark used up as usual, and `shareBp` of the bonus (2500: a quarter) comes back as a reflected hit on the payoff: the shape of Bramble Season's partial reflect (aimed, a Guard beside may take it, Shield Wall, armor and sheet apply, no crit, dodge or cover). Fairer than a rare whole reflect: it is nearly every fight, it is a cost and never a lost reaction, and no single roll decides the run.
- **Tuned with the same single-room study** (B-031): the share is the knob, chance stays 5000. Knock-out share climbs in steps with the share (a burn is lethal only when it is bigger than what the payoff has left), so there is no smooth ramp. 2500 puts the Slag Tyrant a few points under the other room bosses and level with Thorns.
- **Tidepool Finery (`unreflected`)** turns a backlash aside for the creature's first reaction of the fight, as it turns Backdraft aside: its set text ("cannot be reflected") still reads true because the burn is a reflected hit. The die is still drawn, so the rest of the fight draws the same either way.
- **Name and tag.** The glossary entry is "Backlash" (`gimmick-backlash`), distinct from Thorns' "Spikes". Not "Riposte" (a gear line already) and no longer "Reflective shield".
- `RULES.combat.maxHitBp` stays 0 and no dungeon cap was added; the burn takes the cap if it is ever switched on (combat-rules.md 5.5.3).

## 2026-10-22: The dungeon's reflect is split in two: spiky armour (Thorns) and a reflective shield (Molten Shell). The raid is unchanged

ID: `2026-10-22-the-dungeon-s-reflect-is-split` · Status: current

Misa's call, after the balance work in B-002: reflect knocked out a creature in 97 to 100% of the runs that met it (the Thorns room modifier, or the Slag Tyrant's Molten Shell). The first reaction of each round came back whole, uncapped and undodgeable, so players learnt to avoid reactions, which is the opposite of what the game wants. The raid is untouched, because many players fight it together and its balance (the Cinder Wyrm's Backdraft, Bramble Season) was tuned as it is (B-007). Dungeon only:

- **Thorns is spiky armour (`spikes`, `bp`).** Every creature hit that lands on the foe (a turn hit or an ability strike, not an Echo) sends `bp` of that hit's **plain** damage back at the hitter, so a reaction costs the same as a plain hit. The returning hit: no Guard step-in, no dodge, no crit, no cover; Shield Wall, armor and `reflectedBp` apply as for a reflected hit; capped by `maxHitBp`. It can knock a creature out. Starting value 1500, shipped 1000 (10%) after the sim.
- **Molten Shell is a reflective shield (`reflect_chance`, `chanceBp`).** Each reaction a living creature sets off has that chance to be reflected exactly like Backdraft's reflect (combat-rules.md 5.4.1 to 5.4.3), with no once-a-round limit, and the returning hit is capped like a boss hit. One die from the fight's rng per reaction, drawn only while the shield is up, so no other fight draws differently (every stored fight and fixture replays as before). Tidepool Finery (`unreflected`) works against it. Starting value 1000, shipped 500 (5%) after the sim.
- **Why two ops, not one rule.** Misa wants reflect to have its own tag, distinct from Thorns: a glossary entry "Reflective shield" next to "Spikes", and the Thorns room modifier now reads "Every hit on the foe sends some damage back at the hitter."
- **Standing effects write no `gimmick` event.** They do nothing on the boss's turn; the "Next round" forecast names them (Thorns: "every hit pricks the hitter"; Molten Shell: "may reflect a reaction") when there is room after the foe's moves, and the spike hit shows as a number on the hitter, like any boss hit, without a wind-up.
- **The single-hit cap is off today** (`RULES.combat.maxHitBp` 0, Misa 2026-10-15), so "capped like a boss hit" is inert until it is switched on; the engine already applies it to both new hits. With a 50% cap a reflect would no longer be a sure knock-out (B-031 has the numbers for both).
- Old runs and fixtures: the ops are new and the engine's draws are unchanged for fights without them. Stored dungeon runs that hold the old Thorns reflect (embedded in the room) replay exactly as they were fought; only newly built runs get the new content.

## 2026-10-22: Rare rooms: one roll, one pool

ID: `2026-10-22-rare-rooms-one-roll-one-pool` · Status: current

Misa found three separate chances (25%, 20%, 15%) and a pick among the hits too fiddly, and asked for two pools. When a run steps into a mystery room, **one roll** (`RULES.dungeon.rareRoomChanceBp`, 4000 = 40%; the three chances gave about 49% together from depth 3 on and less at the shallow depths, so this is about the same share, a little lower) decides whether it is a rare room. If it is, the room is picked **evenly among the eligible rare rooms**: each keeps its `fromDepth` (vault 1, door 2, chasm 3), the vault's "a fight must lie ahead" filter (`rareEventsAt`), and never one the run already met (`rareSeen`). If none is eligible the room stays the normal one and no dice are drawn.

- `chanceBp` is removed from the `rare` field of an event (`rare: { fromDepth }`, the schema is still strict, so an old `chanceBp` is refused). `rollRareEvent` is now two draws at most (the roll, then the pick), live from the server's rng only. Adding a rare room is one `rare: { fromDepth }`; the odds of the whole pool are the one number in the rules.
- At depth 1 only the vault can come up, so a rare room there is always the vault (before it was 25%); at depth 2 the pool is the door and the vault; from depth 3 each is a third of the rare rooms (about 13% of mystery rooms each). Tests: each room's gate, once per run, the filter, the draw counts (none when nothing is eligible, one on a miss, two on a hit), the 40% share and the even split over 20000 seeded rolls.
- Worker tests that script the dice now push two values for a rare room (the roll, then the index of the pick in content order: chasm 0, door 1, vault 2).

The option text says only "The next fight is epic, with double rewards." (Misa, 2026-10-22: name the promise, not the exact effects, which may change).
- **A marked room wears the epic skull on the map** (`nodeGlyphOf` uses the shown kind) while the mark lasts, with the x2 badge; the ring and the element tint stay the room's own.

## 2026-10-21: The dungeon pays experience per room a creature stood in, and the end screen recaps it

ID: `2026-10-21-the-dungeon-pays-experience-per-room` · Status: current

Misa: "experience should be paid per fight that a creature was in, and the dungeon end screen should show a recap and exp gain for each creature that earned experience... higher for harder fights, a bit for cache rooms but not as much as fights, and as an effect in mystery rooms."

- **The rule** (`RULES.experience.dungeon`, B-028): every creature that stood in the row for a room earns its amount, by kind: fight 32, elite 48, epic (Undercroft) 58, keeper (Undercroft) 58, floor boss 105, cache 10. A lost fight pays nothing, as before. Depth adds nothing. The treat and the day's Boons and Omens multiply the creature's total once at the end (`dungeonExperience(earned, parts)`), so the xp breakdown chip still reads the same.
- **No separate full-clear bonus.** It folds into the boss's amount (35 + 60 = 95 before, 105 now). It only went to the row that beat the boss, which is the row that fought the boss, so the same creatures are paid; the recap shows one "boss" entry instead of a bonus nobody can see. A creature sent home at a camp can never be paid for a boss it did not fight.
- **Numbers** came from the balance sim over 840 runs (B-028): a typical full clear pays +6% (the shortest +15%), a defeat -1% for the fights it played, against today. The first try (30 / 45 / 55 / 55 / 95 / 10) paid 2% and 7% less, so every number was raised a little.
- **Run state.** `RunState.tally` (fights cleared per creature) became `RunState.earned` (per creature: the base total plus counts of fights, elites, epics, keepers, bosses, caches and events that paid). Counts are for the recap; the total is stored as paid, so a rules change mid-run never changes what a creature already earned. A run stored with the old `tally` is read by `parseState`, which prices each fight as a plain fight and one as the boss for a creature in `fullClear` (`earnedFromTally`). Runs are per day and stale ones are closed by the sweeps, so only a run open during the deploy ever takes that path.
- **Mystery rooms** have a new plain effect `xp` (`{ op: 'xp', amount }`, 1 to 60, also allowed inside a gamble branch): every standing creature (not a knocked-out one) earns that amount before the treat and weather. Old Shrine's "Bow and walk on" gives 20 and Mushroom Ring's dance, when it wins, 25. The event description says the exact amount (a content test holds every description to its numbers).
- **The recap.** `DungeonRewards.recap` (`DungeonExperienceRecap`: creature id, name and species id; the counts and base were dropped from the payload the same day) sits next to `experience` (what was paid after boosts and its level changes). It names the creature, so one sent home at a camp, who is not in `team`, can be listed. Only creatures that earned something are paid and listed. The end screen's "Experience" list is one row for each (the shared `XpGain` row) with a short line under the bar ("4 fights, 1 cache, boss"; kinds that did not pay are left out) and a "Went home" tag on a creature not in the row at the end. A run stored before the recap shows its team's rows without the line.
- **Round 2 (Misa): no reason line, just the experience.** The "4 fights, 1 cache, boss" line under each bar is gone (`recapLine`, the `note` prop of `XpGain` / `ExperienceGainRow` and `.xp-note` were removed). The recap in the result keeps only who earned (id, name, species, so a creature sent home at a camp can be listed); run state still counts rooms per creature.
- **Not changed:** treats cost, the level-up and ribbon rules, the lost-fight rule, who stands in the row (a creature knocked out still stood in it for a cache and a won fight, as before).

## 2026-10-21: The Blue Chasm, a rare mystery room with a treasure shown first

ID: `2026-10-21-the-blue-chasm-a-rare-mystery` · Status: current

Misa asked for a rare mystery room: a plank over a chasm, a valuable treasure on the other side, 50/50 to cross or the whole team falls and the run ends. He wanted it kept at 50/50 with a full defeat, and made tempting by the prize. It is `blue-chasm` in `dungeon-events.json`, lore in `docs/compendium/dungeon.md`.

- **The room.** "Cross the plank" is a gamble (`chanceBp: 5000`, in the content, so it can be tuned): win takes the treasure, lose is a `fall`. "Don't risk it" does nothing. Two new event effects: `treasure` (take the treasure the room rolled) and `fall` (every creature at 0 HP, the run ends as a defeat: half the pack kept, `RULES.dungeon.defeatKeepBp`). A `fall` counts as a cost for the "every event has a free option" rule.
- **How rare.** An event may carry `rare: { chanceBp, fromDepth }`. A rare event is never drawn onto a map (the day's map is shared, the rare room is the player's own dice). Each time a run steps into a mystery room it may turn into a rare event: 15% (`chanceBp: 1500`), from depth 3, at most once a run (`rareSeen` in the run state). A run walks about one mystery room, so about one run in six to seven at depth 3 or more meets it (the first depths never do). Drawn live in `applyMove`, not at the map, so it costs nothing in the map code and the day's maps and the simulator's maps are unchanged. The simulator does not play it (no rare room on its maps).
- **The treasure** is rolled when the room opens (`rollTreasure` in `rules/dungeon-treasure.ts`, the live gear rng), stored in the run's pending state and shown in the room before the choice (the actual gear piece, the blueprint base, the supplies, the shards). Reloading cannot reroll it. One kind is drawn evenly among those that can apply to this player:
  - an **Epic gear piece** at the item level of the run's depth (`rollDungeonItem`), tagged with the element of one team creature;
  - a **blueprint** the player does not know, one tier above their best. A base's tier is its `minItemLevel` (1, 5, 10, and the gear sets' 12). The pool is the unknown craftable bases of the nearest tier above the best known one; with nothing unknown above, the highest unknown tier. Not a candidate when they know every blueprint;
  - **two great supplies** of one kind (never a lure);
  - **6 shards** (two per team creature of a team of three, of its element) and **4 glimmer** (`RULES.dungeon.treasure`; round 2 raised it from 3 and 2, Misa, 2026-10-21).
  - Not in the table yet: **a creature of a species the player lacks.** Granting a creature from a run needs its own path (collection rules, first talent, server firsts, duplicate card, a creature find on the end screen), so it is a follow-up.
- **Delivery.** A won treasure is stored in the run (`treasures`) and paid at the run's end by the run's own grant (`dungeon:<day>:<attempt>`), so it is paid once and a retry returns the stored result. A run that ends in a defeat wins none of it: an item cannot be halved, so unlike the pack it is all or nothing. The end screen shows a won treasure as finds (gear, blueprint, supplies, shards) and a lost one as "Treasure lost". The fall itself takes no treasure.
- **Round 2 (Misa, 2026-10-21): inspect the prize first.** The Epic piece is fully rolled when the room opens (base, element tag, level, lines) and kept in the run's state; the room's event view carries its `card` (`previewItemView` in `gear-access.ts`, built by the same code as the Workshop's cards, so the client computes nothing), validated by the client parse. Tapping or hovering the treasure row opens the full `ItemCard` with no Equip (`FindItem` takes `card`; a `card` is never equippable, only an owned `id` is). The grant at the end pays that stored item, not a re-roll. The card is built when the view is made, so a waiting room saved before this change shows the card too; if its base is gone from the content the row is simply plain. Shards prize raised to 6 shards and 4 glimmer. The 'creature you don't have' prize is deferred, very low priority (`docs/roadmap.md`).
- **Old runs.** A saved run without `treasures` and `rareSeen` loads as having none. A mystery room waiting from before the change has no treasure and answers as before.
- **Server side checks.** The event is picked by the server, the odds and the treasure are rolled by the server with live randomness, and the client sends only the option (0 or 1) as for every event.

**Internal names stay.** In code, API fields and stored run JSON, `floor` is still a row (step) of a map, `floors` the steps of a map, `level` an Undercroft floor (1 to 3, 0 is the day's map), `campFloor` and node ids (`f2l1`, `u1f0l0`) are unchanged, and `keeper` stays in function, file and workbench stage names (`isKeeper`, `openKeeperChest`, `dungeon-act-keeper.json`). ## 2026-10-18: The Undercroft (a deeper, optional level) and the carved door

ID: `2026-10-18-the-undercroft-a-deeper-optional-level` · Status: current

- **Why:** runs should grow longer as players progress, by the player's choice (Misa). Design in `docs/design/dungeon-paths.md`, "The Undercroft".
- **Server shape:** the main boss at depth 8 or more does not end the run. Depth +1 is written to `dungeon_progress` at once (`MAX`), and the chest opens: its gear, shard, glimmer and supply are paid right then under the grant key `dungeon:<day>:<attempt>:chest` (an Undercroft keeper's under `...:u<level>`), and its Essence and scrap go into the pack. A defeat below or a run left open can therefore never undo the depth or the banked gear. The run's end pays the pack through the usual ledger keys and adds what the chests paid to the summary's rewards, without paying it again.
- **Outcome names stay `cleared | home | defeat`.** A run that beat the main boss ends `cleared` when it heads home or is closed at a safe point (the descent card, an Undercroft camp), and `defeat` when it is lost below; its summary still says `cleared: true` and the run row `cleared = 1`, so the first-day retry and the clear counts treat it as a win. `deepest` counts the levels cleared. A run that is closed at a camp before the boss stays `home`.
- **The carved door** (Misa's refinement, same day): the player chooses nothing. Stepping through adds ONE room of a random kind (fight, elite, mystery, cache, camp or epic, never a boss; odds in `RULES.dungeon.undercroft.markOdds`) drawn with live randomness, the player is only told what it could be, and the history says only that a room was added. The room is a floor of one room before the keeper (camps last). With at most one door a map, the cap of 2 marks (`maxMarks`) is reachable only if a second source appears; it is enforced anyway.
- **The door is drawn last** in `generateDungeonMap` and only when asked (`{ doors: true }`; the worker asks, the simulator does not until it plays the Undercroft, which keeps its pinned numbers), so a map without a door is exactly what the same stream drew before doors. Below depth 8 its view is a mystery room named "Quiet room" (so the client needs no special case) and it plays as nothing, so the shared map shows nothing a player cannot use.
- **Undercroft maps** have floors of two rooms in lanes 0 and 2 that lead straight up their lane (a real choice of path, no crossing), so the player's first tap chooses a lane until the next single-room floor.
- **Floors:** the first level has `min(5, 2 + (landmarks passed - 1))` floors of two rooms, where a run at depth d has passed `floor((d - 1) / 7)` landmarks, plus the marked floors and the keeper; a deeper level (a cracked floor) has 2 plus the keeper.
- **Starting values for the simulator to tune:** epic fight HP +60% and Attack +30% of the depth-scaled foe (an elite is +40% and +20%), door on 35% of maps.

- **End report order:** the rewards first (the orb reveal, Bait and Essence icons, parts and gear), then a strip of the three rooms (the foe's sprite; a cleared room is grey with a green CLEARED stamp, the lost room has DEFEAT in red with the foe's HP left, a room never reached is dim with LOCKED; states are classes and CSS filters, no inline styles), then the team in its row with `340 / 391 HP` under each creature (a knocked-out one is grey with a skull and `0 / 391 HP` in red). The role and element chips are gone from the end report; the popover below shows them.
- **The small-creature popover:** one component (`SmallCreature`) opens one popover (the glossary layer, `StagePopover` and the glossary's body) with the creature's name, level, role and element as tags. Tapping a tag switches the same popover to that glossary entry, with a back control; Escape goes back first and then closes. The two faces are a small state machine in `creature-popover.ts` (tested). It is used in the dungeon end report and on the dungeon stage, on other players' creatures on the raid's live stage and lobby preview, on every creature in the raid replay, and on the explorer's portrait in Expeditions. On the live stage your own creature still opens the fire popover, which now ends with the role and element tags. **Left out on purpose:** the Placement strip, the dungeon team picker and its list (their sprites are buttons that select or are dragged; a popover tap would collide with tap-to-select and with drag, and the list already shows tags next to each creature), the raid after-action "Top creatures" (the stats carry no level), and the Collection cards and Workshop (not small display-only sprites). Expedition catches are species only, so they have no level to show either.

**Reasoning.** One row is easier to read than three stacked one-creature zones and makes the order the whole formation; an adjacent Guard gives the order a real choice without changing how raids work; keeping the numbers and moving only words and one generic option keeps the engine free of dungeon special cases.

## 2026-10-13: Daily dungeon v1 (engine, content, server and balance model)

ID: `2026-10-13-daily-dungeon-v1-engine-content-server` · Status: current

The engine, content, server and simulator half of `design/daily-dungeon.md` is in; the UI comes next (`apps/activity/src/dungeon-types.ts` and the `dungeon-*.json` fixtures are what it builds against). All 37 simulator targets (33 old, 4 new) pass on the full run with seed `leet`.

**Defaults held for the open questions** (until Misa says otherwise): one run per guild-local day per player, plus one free retry on the first day a player ever ran, **only when that first run was lost** (a cleared first run gets no retry); raid creatures are used freely with no locking, and creatures on an expedition or a workshop job may go too (the run is instant, as raids allow); no leaderboard and no Discord post yet, but every run is kept (summary and rewards for good, inputs and events for 90 days) so either can be added.

- **Engine.** `FightSlot.hp` is an optional starting HP (capped at the creature's maximum, which includes the Guard bonus; 0 starts the creature knocked out, and a fight where everyone starts knocked out plays no rounds). `FightResult` gained `hpStart`, `hpEnd` and `hpMax` by creature id, which is what chaining needs; nothing else in the fight changed (existing tests, the prefix property tests and the golden fight are untouched; stored raid results now carry the three small maps). `simulateDungeon` plays the rooms in order from one seed, each room from its own seeded stream (`<seed>:room:<n>`), carries HP, keeps knocked-out creatures out, and stops at the first lost room. The rooms of a run are stored in full (foe and modifier definitions included), so a replay never reads content again. A solo row of 3 needed nothing: the zones of a row of three are one slot each.
- **Room modifiers are folds, not engine features.** A modifier is content: a list of effects (`team_stat` for the team's Attack, HP or speed by element and/or role, `foe_stat`, `team_crit`, `foe_gimmick`, `turn_order`) applied to the room's fight input before the fight (stats, foe HP and Attack, the luck config, the gimmick list, `turnOrderOverride`). The fight engine has no dungeon special cases and needs no mutator.
- **Content.** `dungeon-foes.json`: 8 foes (two per element, some with a gimmick: Sea Mist, Flare Up, Thorn Snap, Root Grab, Zap Dive, Buzz Cloud) and 4 room bosses (Drowned Warden, Slag Tyrant with a reflecting shell, Bramble Matron, Coil Baron), each with an element, turn order, gimmicks, rounds (6 for a foe, 8 for a boss) and a flavour line. A foe reuses the boss pieces (gimmicks, turn order, crit bonus) in its own schema and is turned into a boss for the fight. `room-modifiers.json`: Flooded, Scorched, Overgrown, Stormy (an element hits 20% harder), Dark (+5 crit points), Thorns (the foe reflects the first reaction each round), Brittle (foe -20% HP, +25% Attack) and Fog (slot order). Gimmick ops are the existing ones, so no new gimmick glossary entries; each modifier and the terms dungeon, depth, room modifiers, room boss, landmark and streak have glossary entries (section "Rewards", since a tenth How to play section would need page art), and the coverage check requires them. The scrap, shard and glimmer lines of the part entries now mention the dungeon.
- **Rules (`RULES.dungeon`).** A run has 3 rooms (two foes, then a room boss) and a team of 3. Foe HP and Attack at depth d are the content numbers times a base strength (HP 130%, Attack 100%) times `10000 + step x n + curve x n^2` basis points, with n = d - 1: HP step 3000 and curve 300, Attack step 2000 and curve 150, depths above 60 count as 60. Every 7th depth is a landmark. Per cleared room: Bait 1, 1 and 1; Essence 1 and scrap 1 from the boss room; the boss room also drops a shard of the boss's element at 40%. A full clear has a 10% gear chance (rare 18% and epic 2% at depth 1, +0.4 and +0.2 points per depth, capped at 45% and 18%); a fully cleared landmark pays Essence 2, scrap 2 and a glimmer and its gear drop is certain (+20 and +6 points on the rare and epic odds). Streaks count days in a row in a cycle of 7: the 3rd day pays 1 scrap, the 7th 1 Essence and 1 scrap; a missed day starts the count at 1 again and the depth is kept. A landmark depth is only passed once per player (depth never goes down), so landmark chests are rare.
- **Server.** Migration 18: `dungeon_runs` (one row per run; UNIQUE on (player, day, attempt) and on (player, key); the seed, the stored input and result, the summary and the rewards), `dungeon_progress` (depth, streak, last and first day) and `dungeon_days` (the seed of the day's modifiers, drawn once from crypto on the first look). `GET /api/dungeon/today` is the day's state with the three modifiers (from the day seed, which only decides modifiers); `POST /api/dungeon/run` validates, draws the run seed from crypto at that moment, rolls the rooms from it, simulates every room, and stores and pays in ONE transaction; `GET /api/dungeon/run/:id` is the owner's full replay (404 for anyone else's, the same as for a run that does not exist). No answer carries a seed, and the seed cannot be derived from anything seen before (it is a fresh draw; the day seed is a separate draw). A retry with the same key returns the stored run (a changed body under the same key, or a key a ledger spend or a gear action used, is `key_conflict`). Gear folds into the slots with the same `loadCreatures` as raids, so a stored run keeps its stats whatever happens to the gear later. Payouts are idempotent per (player, day, attempt): ledger keys `dungeon:<day>:<attempt>:bait|essence` and gear grants `dungeon:<day>:<attempt>` (a full bag turns an item into parts), and the streak bonus once per day (`dungeon:<day>:streak`). The day is the guild-local calendar day, resolved once per request from the guild timezone and passed down (tests cover both DST changes in Europe/Helsinki, a 23 and a 25 hour day, and Los Angeles). The alarm empties the inputs and events of runs older than 90 days.
- **Request shape.** `team` is 3 ids and `formation` the zones (`front`, `middle`, `back`) each once, where `formation[i]` is the zone of `team[i]`; `abilities.basic` is the basic ability with a round (or null for the default round) and a zone for Shield Wall or an element for Re-mark; `abilities.creatures` lists rounds for creature abilities (every unlocked creature ability, level 10 or more, is in the run, and an unlisted one fires at its default round). Abilities fire once in every room. Errors: `invalid_body` (wrong count, bad key, unknown keys), `duplicate_creature`, `invalid_formation`, `invalid_abilities`, `invalid_id`, `not_found` (not the caller's creature or run), `already_run`, `key_conflict`. A player needs 3 creatures; with fewer, `today` says `need_creatures`.
- **Analytics.** `dungeon_run` (depth, attempt, rooms cleared, cleared, landmark, the modifiers, the team's species and roles, how many wear gear) and `dungeon_reward` (depth, Bait, Essence, scrap, shards, glimmer, gear count, landmark, streak bonus day), content ids and numbers only, one of each per run that was played (a replay or a refused run logs nothing); the dungeon's gear also logs a `gear_drop` with source `dungeon`. The privacy scan covers them.
- **Balance model (`packages/sim`).** Every player with 3 creatures runs the dungeon on each day they check in (casuals on their leet days) with their 3 strongest creatures in the default row (`autoPlace`), Rally as the basic ability and every creature ability at its default round; the day's modifiers are shared, the first-day retry is played, and rewards are paid as the worker pays them. The model uses the game's own pure functions and the engine's `simulateDungeon`. New targets: depth 1 cleared in a player's first 7 days at least 80% (measured 89.7%); a median daily player gains 1-2 depths per week from the end of week 2 to the end of week 8 (1.83); dungeon Essence at most 40% of a daily player's Essence income (3.8%); gear share of a daily player's power at week 8 at most 20% with the dungeon's gear and parts (12.9%, against 12.0% without the dungeon).
- **How depth was tuned.** Daily players climb about a depth a day until the foes catch up with their power, then slowly: median daily depth 5 after week 1, 8 after 2, 15 after 4, 19 after 8 and 21 after 12 (casual 12 and 16 after 8 and 12 weeks, since they run twice a week). Clear rate by depth for a daily player runs from 86% at depth 1 to about 50-60% at depths 5 to 11, 20% at depth 14 and 10% at depth 19. The first version had foes growing 12% a depth, which gave a very sharp wall (depth 30 in a week or two, then nothing); the second compounded and still stalled; a straight line left depth 2 too hard for week-1 teams (46% clear). The shipped curve is gentle first and steeper later, which kept week-1 players able to win (depth 2 to 5 clear 73% to 48% for dailies) and the pace at about 1.8 depths a week through week 8. "About 1-2 per week" is measured from the end of week 2 to the end of week 8, because the opening week is a depth a day for everybody; growth after week 8 is slower (the median daily player gains under 1 a week), because power growth slows then.
- **The rewards are small on purpose.** The raid targets were already thin and the dungeon's income feeds the same economy: Bait buys levels and Essence and scrap buy evolutions, upgrades and crafts. With the first reward numbers (Bait about 90 a week, Essence 11, scrap 25 for a daily player) the 15-player finale win rate went from 51% to 74%, Crafting level 7 against a limit of 6, and setup credit under its minimum. About 14 Bait a week on its own (and nothing else) already took the 15-player finale from 51% to 55%. The shipped rewards give a daily player about 14 Bait, 3 Essence and 4 scrap a week and 0.3 gear items, which still moves the raids: typical finale win rate 49.2% to 55.1%, 15-player finale 51.0% to 63.0% (limit 65%), Phase 2 clear 70.0% to 77.3%. Boss HP and gear numbers were left alone, as asked. If Misa wants the dungeon to feel richer, the raids need their boss HP retuned in the same step.
- **Limits of the check.** The 15-player finale (63.0% of 65%) and Crafting level (6 of 6) are close to their limits; as before, a different seed can miss thin targets. The quick run was not used. Several small reward changes flipped the thin fairness targets (lowest creature contribution, boss spread) by noise, so the shipped numbers are one of the few of about twenty candidates that passed everything, not a robust optimum.
- **Not done here.** The Activity UI (Dungeon tab, pickers, playback, loot reveal, How to play entries), a leaderboard, the Discord post, blueprints as dungeon drops, sprites for foes. `docs/design/daily-dungeon.md` has the new status line.
