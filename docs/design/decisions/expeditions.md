## 2026-10-21: The bag may overflow; a full bag warns where you set off

ID: `2026-10-21-the-bag-may-overflow-a-full` · Status: current · Amends: `2026-10-13-gear-and-crafting-v1-server-and`

Misa found a dungeon find turned into parts because his bag was full, with no warning beforehand. It felt like a loss the game never explained. The new rule:

- **A reward that arrives while the bag is under the cap puts every item in**, even past the cap (58 + 4 = 62 / 60). The choice is made once per grant, before any of its items go in (`grantOnce`, `fits`).
- **A reward that arrives while the bag is full** (items plus crafts still to claim at or over the cap) turns its gear into the dismantle yield, as before (`converted`).
- **A dungeon run decides at its start** (`RunState.bagFull`): the boss chest on the way and the run's end follow the bag as it was when the run began, so a chest that fills the bag never costs the end of the same run. A state stored before has no flag and decides at each grant.
- **A finished craft always goes in.** It held its place when it started, and a grant may have filled the bag past the cap meanwhile. The old "cannot happen" refusal at the claim is gone, so a paid craft is never stuck.
- Starting a craft and the dev tools' item grant still refuse when the bag is full: those are deliberate actions.
- **The warning** (`ui/BagFullNote`): where the player sets off (the dungeon's Enter, the send screen, the raid board while it can change) a gold-edged line says "Your bag is full (62 / 60). Gear you find becomes parts." with a Workshop button. It never blocks. The Workshop's bag count turns red at or over the cap, with one line under it. The results line now names the reason: "Your bag was full, so Bog Charm became parts."
- Expeditions and the raid decide at the grant (their claim or finale), since there is no single start state to stamp. The warning is still right: while the bag is full, gear found becomes parts.

## 2026-10-21: One line per supply, its strength word anchored to a reference trip

ID: `2026-10-21-one-line-per-supply-its-strength` · Status: current · Amends: `2026-10-21-supplies-are-explained-by-tier-words`

Misa did not like a supply's effect split over several lines on the send screen, and did like blurbs that refer to a reference trip.

- **One line per supply.** The supply card (send screen and Workshop alike) shows only its blurb. The per-category "on this trip" lines are gone. The Expected haul strip's "+" marks stay: they describe the whole kit on the trip being sent, and "at least double with your supplies" there can differ from a blurb word because the blurb speaks for the reference trip.
- **Reference trip** (`REFERENCE_TRIP` next to `tripBoost`): 12 hours at Ember Caves (the economy study's reference place), a payoff explorer, no stars, no twist. A supply's main category is lure creatures, map everything (measured on creatures), lantern deeper-area creatures against the plain trip's creatures, satchel parts (`referenceBoost`).
- **The rule:** a blurb's strength word is the bracket of that boost on the reference trip (slightly more, more, many/much more, double; lanterns: slight, fair, strong, very strong chance). A test in `trip-boost.test.ts` holds every blurb to it, so tuning cannot make the words lie.
- **Rewording, no tuning.** The map and satchel tiers did not spread over the brackets (Inked Map +20%, Trail Atlas +46%; Sturdy Satchel and Explorer's Pack both at least double). Raising the map scalars to separate them broke the economy targets (the 12 hour and day pay bands), so the blurbs were reworded instead: Inked Map "slightly more of everything, and then some", Trail Atlas "more of everything", Small Satchel "More parts", Sturdy "Double parts, sometimes glimmer", Explorer's Pack "Double parts, glimmer too". `MAX_SUPPLY_BLURB` rose from 50 to 60 for the Inked Map.
- **Final wording (Misa).** Maps are worded relative to each other, not to the brackets (the one documented exception, tested as a rising ladder): Map Scrap "A good route: a bit more of everything", Inked Map "A great route: more of everything", Trail Atlas "A fantastic route: much more of everything". Satchels: Sturdy "Double parts with a tiny chance of glimmer", Explorer's Pack "Triple parts and a small chance of glimmer" (glimmer is a chance, never promised: tested); "triple" is allowed only from +200% on the reference trip (`TRIPLE_FROM_BP`). "And then some" is gone.

## 2026-10-21: Supplies are explained by tier words and a boost bracket, never by counts

ID: `2026-10-21-supplies-are-explained-by-tier-words` · Status: current, amended by `2026-10-21-one-line-per-supply-its-strength`

Misa wants supplies explained honestly without exact numbers. Two parts:

- **Tier blurbs** follow one ladder per kind (lures: "Slightly more creatures, and likelier new species" / "More ..." / "Many more ..."; maps: "A better route: slightly more of everything" up to "much more of everything"; lanterns: slight / fair / strong chance of a deeper creature; satchels: "Slightly more parts" up to "Many more parts, glimmer too"). A map adds ticks to every table of a trip (creatures, parts, gear, glimmer, blueprints, supplies, journal, XP), so "everything" is true; the text never says the trip lasts longer. The glossary entries for the four kinds say the same.
- **Boost bracket per category, for the trip being sent.** `expectedTrip` (packages/game `rules/trip-ev.ts`) is now the ONE exact expected-value implementation; the balance simulator (`packages/sim/src/trip-ev.ts`) calls it with its reference trip, so both agree. `tripBoost` (`rules/trip-boost.ts`) returns, per category (creatures, new species, deeper creatures, parts by worth, gear, XP), the relative boost of the kit over the same trip with no supplies. Brackets: below +3% nothing; to +25% "slightly more"; to +60% "more"; to +100% "a lot more"; from +100% "at least double". Deeper creatures a trip would not bring at all without a lantern are measured against the plain trip creatures. The send screen shows the lines on a packed supply's card ("Parts: at least double on this trip") and the Expected haul strip's "+" marks repeat one to four times by bracket. Not modelled: the day's Boon on the strip, Keen nose gear, milestones (small, and they cancel in a ratio). A Small Satchel is "at least double" parts on 2 h and "more" on 12 h: short trips gain most from supplies, which is true to the maths.
- **The Workshop card shows only the blurb** (no trip is chosen there; an assumed trip would mislead).

## 2026-10-21: Richer rank gifts, and "Set out with" on the claim screen

ID: `2026-10-21-richer-rank-gifts-and-set-out` · Status: current · Amends: `2026-10-13-ux-pass-1-expeditions-rank-up`

Misa asked for two changes on the claim screen.

**Rank gifts** (`RULES.expeditions.rank.gifts`) were tiny (rank 2: 2 Bait and 1 scrap). They are now 10 Bait and 5 scrap at rank 2 and grow from there; from rank 4 a gift adds shards, from rank 5 one gear piece, with rising rarity odds (rank 6 and up rare or better, ranks 9 and 10 with a real epic chance). The full table is in `expeditions-v2.md`. Decisions:

- Shards and the gear piece are of the **explorer's element**, not the destination's. Ranking up is the creature's achievement, and it gives a reason to explore with several creatures. The gear piece uses `rollExpeditionItem` with `{ element: explorer's element, crossroads: false }` at the item level of the rank reached; the rarity is picked from the gift's own odds (`gearOddsBp`, scalars that sum to 10000).
- The gift is still granted once per (creature, rank) through the grant ledger (`rank:<creature>:<rank>`), inside the claim's transaction, with the live gear RNG. A replayed claim returns the stored result and rolls nothing. A full bag turns the piece into its dismantle yield (shown as "did not fit"), like every other grant. The gift appears in analytics as a `gear_drop` of source `rank_gift`.
- The rank track (the ladder in the Expeditions screen) says "3 shards of its element" and "a rare gear piece", since it is for every creature.
- The numbers were set by Misa's brief, not a balance pass. The sim grants the same shards and gear (`rollRankGiftItem`) so the economy targets still see them. See B-027 in `docs/balance/log.md`.

**Set out with.** The claim card shows what the trip started with, before "Brought home": the length, each supply packed (its name opens the supply's glossary card), a treat, the day's Boon and Omen that changed the trip, the weekly twist and the push-on goal. The trip view (`ExpeditionView`) gained `weather` (`{ boon, omen }`, id and name; `null` when no weather was stored: the trip had none, was sent before the weather, or the stored value is damaged). Everything else was already in the view. A trip that was out at deploy shows what it has; absent fields just do not show.

## 2026-10-20: Expedition trips pay in ticks

ID: `2026-10-20-expedition-trips-pay-in-ticks` · Status: current, amended by `2026-10-20-a-recall-pays-explorer-xp-too`, `2026-10-20-the-lantern-gets-a-bonus-deep` · Amends: `2026-10-13-expeditions-v2-numbers-set-by-simulation`

Player feedback: a 12 h trip was no better than six 2 h ones, rewards were tiny, supplies felt useless, catches were rare and nobody could craft. Misa's answer, built the same day (`docs/design/expeditions-v2.md`, "Ticks"):

- **A finished trip has ticks by length:** 2 h is 2, 6 h is 7, 12 h is 15, 24 h is 32 (`RULES.expeditions.lengths.<l>.ticks`). They run ahead of the hours, so long trips win; players are only told "longer trips are better". Ticks are nominal: explorer rank shortens the time, not the ticks.
- **Each tick** gives 20 explorer XP (`RULES.expeditions.tick.xp`, boosts multiply the total) and rolls every table on its own, in basis points per tick: creature 1250, scrap 5000, a shard 600, a gear piece 513 (rolled like a push-on drop, rarity 8500 / 1350 / 150), glimmer 30, blueprint 40, supply 200, journal page 70 (about 0.1 pages for a 12 h trip, as before). No caps: lucky streaks are fine. All modifiers (gear lines, talents, weather, twists, stars, milestones, Guard, Setter) scale these scalars.
- **Shards follow the place**, never the explorer; a place without an element (Old Crossroads, Festival Grounds) draws each shard's element evenly of the five. "Parts" means every crafting ingredient: scrap, shards and glimmer.
- **The catch pity is gone** (`newAfter`, `creatureEvery`; the two counters stay in `player_progress`, unused, no migration). The dupe shield and the first-expedition Zipmouse stay. The `catch_pity` analytics event keeps its id but is no longer recorded.
- **Recall** pays the ticks of the whole nominal hours already spent (never the bonus ticks), each rolled normally, and no fixed parts, XP, journal or stats. **Push-on** hours are ticks, one per hour, on top of the goal's reward; a goal's `catchRolls` are extra creature-only rolls at the tick's chance (the old EV, now fairer on short trips). Weekly twist `rolls` are extra ticks. Because the extra hours are worth ticks, the risky goals' odds were re-derived so each is a fair gamble again (about 1.00 in the simulator's worth table).
- **Supplies have a fixed part and a per-tick part**, all in `supplies.json`: Map (extra ticks: fixed plus a share of the ticks, fractions roll as a chance), Lure (a fixed chance of one bonus creature that surely comes, a little more chance per tick, `newBp` as before), Lantern (a deeper chance per catch plus a fixed chance that the first creature comes from the deeper area), Satchel (fixed scrap, shards and a chance of glimmer, plus a share more per tick and more supply finds). Lures cost less Bait (6 / 15 / 30). Blurbs say what each does (issue #47). A creature is worth 15 / 40 / 90 Bait in the simulator's value table.
- **Server:** the trip's `rolls` column stores its whole ticks (length, twist, day, gear); the map's ticks and the lure's and lantern's chances are rolled at the claim with live randomness. A trip in flight at deploy is paid by its length (`max(stored, length ticks)`; the old stored catch rolls were always below it). No migration (version 36 is another change's).
- **Simulator:** plays the tick rules; `node packages/sim/scripts/trip-yield.mjs` prints what a trip brings by length and supply. The sim's headline pins (`run.test.ts`) are not re-pinned here.

## 2026-10-20: Push-on extra time starts at the decision

ID: `2026-10-20-push-on-extra-time-starts-at` · Status: current · Amends: `2026-10-13-expeditions-v2-numbers-set-by-simulation`

Misa's call. The extra hours of a push-on used to count from the end of the trip, so a player who opened the app late found it already over and the creature acted as if it had pushed on all along. Now `push_started_at` is the moment of the choice and `push_ends_at` is that plus the goal's extra hours: pushing on carries its price in time, and the creature stays busy until then. Claiming earlier still means coming home (the push-on is skipped). Trips already pushing on keep their stored times (no migration). This replaces the "loses no time" line of the push-on entry above.

## 2026-10-18: Weather numbers a player can read

ID: `2026-10-18-weather-numbers-a-player-can-read` · Status: current · Amends: `2026-10-17-boons-and-omens-built-the-valley`

Misa's forecast showed "31.2% more parts" and "Crit chance +3.7 pt": a Boon or Omen's number times the day's strength (x1.25, x1.5, x2.5) gave odd decimals. `scaleStrength` (`packages/game/src/rules/weather.ts`) now rounds the scaled number towards zero to whole percent or points from 10 up and to halves below (31.25% is 31%, 3.75 pt is 3.5 pt, 2.5% stays). ## 2026-10-18: The last three weathers are live: Kind Rain, Morning Haze, Bramble Season

ID: `2026-10-18-the-last-three-weathers-are-live` · Status: current · Amends: `2026-10-17-boons-and-omens-built-the-valley`

Finishes step 6 of `gear-and-creatures-v2.md` (section 14): all 10 Boons and all 10 Omens are in the valley. Technical decisions by the implementer, for the lead's review. No migration, no change to `RULES`, no raid or dungeon number tuned (another branch is reworking the dungeon).

- **Three new operations in the closed set** of `weather-schema.ts`, each with its words as a template: `roundHeal` (scaled, `bp` of max HP a round), `skipFirstTurn` (fixed, no number) and `bramble` (scaled, `bp` share of a reaction's bonus). Kind Rain's trip half uses the existing `tripParts` (25% more parts, `places` Tidepools and Verdant Hollow) and Morning Haze's the existing `tripTime` (+10%), so trips needed no new rule, only the data; the Verdant Hollow id is checked at load against the destinations as before. The `planned` status stays in the schema for later ideas (a planned entry is still never drawn); nothing ships as planned any more.
- **Engine hooks are optional fields**, so absent means exactly today's fight and the replay fixture and every stored fight are untouched (a test replays 24 random fights with the fields absent, empty and zero and compares byte for byte): `FightInput.rainHealBp`, `skipFirstTurn` (creature ids) and `brambleBp`; `DungeonRunInput.rainHealBp`, `hazeLeft` and `brambleBp`, copied into every room. They are built by `weatherSky(day)` (`@creatures/game`) and `skyFightFields` / `skyRunFields` (`packages/engine/src/sky.ts`) in the Worker's raid stage and dungeon run and in the sim, and stored with the fight input like the rest of the day. None of them draws a die, so no existing die moves.
- **Kind Rain (`rainHealBp`).** At round start, after the Supports' Mend and before finale abilities, every living creature heals `floor(max HP x bp / 10000)`, changed by its own `healReceivedBp` (so Long Shadows' "healing is 25% weaker" weakens the rain as well, as the Omen says), never above what is missing and never a revival. One `sky_heal` event per round lists who was really healed (a per-creature event would flood a 15-creature log); nothing is written when nobody needed it. It rains in enrage rounds too (the sky is not a boss move). Scaled by the Boon's strength: 2% at x1, 2.5% at rank 6 and 3% at rank 10.
- **Morning Haze (`skipFirstTurn`).** The named creatures skip the first turn they reach alive (a `skip` event with reason `haze`). A first turn that was silenced anyway is the lost turn (the silence counts down as usual and the haze is spent on it), so the haze never costs two turns; a creature that starts the fight knocked out never spends it. "First creature of each row" is the leftmost slot of each zone that has anyone (`firstOfEachRow`; a row of one or two is all Middle, so one creature). In the dungeon the row of three has one slot per zone, so the engine's zones would skip all three: the dungeon names only the left creature (`team[0]`) and does it in every room (each room is a fresh fight, as Fair Winds' first hit is per fight). The skip is fixed, not scaled (a count of turns). It draws no die: the skipped creature's dice are drawn and unused, so every other creature rolls what it always did.
- **Bramble Season (`brambleBp`), the rule chosen.** The first reaction each round (by a creature still standing) sends `bp` of its bonus damage back as a reflected hit on the creature that set it off, meaning the payoff creature as for Backdraft (`combat-rules.md` 1.17, 5.4.5); the boss takes the rest of the bonus, and the setup credit is worked out from what the boss took. The returning part goes through `hurt(..., 'reflect', 'reflect')`, the same path as Backdraft's, so a Guard that can steps in, then Shield Wall, armor, `reflectedBp`, the ward and `takenBp` all count: the gear line "Backdraft ward" works against it with no new code. It is a share, not the whole bonus (Backdraft's boss move still reflects 100% of its reaction): when Backdraft reflected the round's first reaction, Bramble does nothing more that round. Scaled by the Omen's strength like every Omen number. **The share is 12% at x1 (lead's call after the sim):** it appears from rank 6, where it is 18% (24% at rank 10, 30% at rank 14), round numbers at every strength. The design's 30% cost 5 points of finale wins at x1 and 17.8 at rank 14, against 0 to 2 for every other Omen; at 12% it costs 0.6 at x1, 1.3 at rank 6 and 5.0 at rank 14, still the hardest Omen at high ranks, as a late-game Omen should be. The number is `bp` in `weather.json`.
- **In the log and on the stage.** `describeEvent`: "Kind Rain mends A (+20 HP) and B (+20 HP).", "X is lost in the Morning Haze and misses its first turn.", and the reaction line gains "Bramble Season throws 95 of it (30%) back at X." (names as text). Playback counts `sky_heal` as a visible beat item; the stage shows green numbers on every creature healed and a small "Hazy" over a hazed creature (not the silence mark); the client's HP bars count the rain (`creatureHpAt`) and the client accepts the new event type. The wall calendar, forecast card and "Sky today" line read the data and needed no change. Workbench: the sky entries have presets for each of the three, and the stage page has a scenario for each.
- **Forecast.** `forecast.ts` plays the same fight input, so it predicts a day's rain, haze and brambles wherever the input carries them (no change to the file).
- **Tests.** The engine tests (`sky.test.ts`) cover each hook, "absent = identical", the dice, the dungeon's leftmost rule, Backdraft's interplay, the Guard and the Shield Wall; the game tests cover `weatherSky`, the schema and the draw (Haze never meets Fair Winds, Bramble Season never before rank 6); the Worker tests check the stored raid and dungeon inputs; the client tests check the cues and the HP.

## 2026-10-17: Boons and Omens built: the valley's weather (migration 31)

ID: `2026-10-17-boons-and-omens-built-the-valley` · Status: current, amended by `2026-10-18-the-last-three-weathers-are-live`, `2026-10-18-weather-numbers-a-player-can-read`

Step 6 of the build order in `gear-and-creatures-v2.md` (section 14), per section 10 and the 2026-10-16 decisions (the wall calendar, "lean UI", Market Day's limits). Technical decisions by the lead and implementer. The numbers are starting values in `RULES.weather`; the sim reports what they do to the raid (`docs/balance/log.md` B-017) and the raid was not tuned.

- **The draw.** `drawWeather` in `@creatures/game` is pure: it takes the injected RNG and `{ rank, festival, previousOmen, wildAllowed }` and returns the Boon, the Omen (or none), whether it is wild, and the strengths. The Worker draws a day ahead with crypto randomness at the moment it is needed (never a seed): `Weather.ensure()` makes sure today's and tomorrow's days are stored, today's first, so tomorrow's Omen knows today's. It runs on every authenticated request, on the alarm (a weather candidate at the 13:37 reset joins the guild's alarm computation) and after a dev clock change, and is a string comparison when the day is known. Table `weather_days` (day, boon, omen, wild, boon_bp, omen_bp, rank, week_id, festival, drawn_at) keeps 30 days (`RULES.weather.keepDays`). The rank is the Boss Rank at the moment of the draw, so a day drawn on Thursday keeps Thursday's strength.
- **Rules of the draw (section 10.1).** One Boon a day, never one that cancels the day's Omen (cancels are symmetric and checked when the content loads). An Omen from Boss Rank 3, never the same one two days running, none in a Festival week (Boons only). A wild Omen (Stormfront) from rank 10, with chance `wildChanceBp` (20%), at most one a raid week, only for a day that has not started yet (so it is always known a day ahead), never in a Festival week. The strengths are the `RULES.weather.tiers` table (x1 from rank 1, Omens from 3, x1.25 and x1.5 from 6, x1.5 and x2 from 10, x1.5 and x2.5 from 14). A rank 1 or 2 server never meets an Omen.
- **Content is data.** `weather.json` (10 Boons, 10 Omens), schema-validated at load (`loadWeather`): every effect is one of a closed set of operations (a sheet field, linger, a reaction bonus, crit, foe crit, foe attack, extra boss attacks, trip time, parts, shards, supplies, extra catch rolls, rarity, journal chance, Moonlit Reef open, experience, Workshop time, Market Day, dungeon scrap) with its words as a template (`{n}` is the figure at the day's strength). The words on the calendar come from the same data and the same scaling, so the numbers shown are the numbers applied.
- **Where it touches.** Only through the existing hooks, no engine rewrite. The raid stage and every dungeon run: the engine's `SlotMods` sheet (merged into each creature's, guests too, clamped to the engine's ranges by `mergeMods`), `luck` (crit chance, foe crit chance) and `bossAttacks` (a new optional field of the dungeon run input, as the fight already had it). Trips: time, parts, shard and supply chances, extra catch rolls, rarity, journal chance and the Reef. Workshop jobs: time and experience. Creature experience: a percentage on top and the treat's multiplier. Dungeon payout: scrap a room.
- **Stored with the thing, so claims are deterministic.** A trip stores the day's effects when it is sent (`expeditions.weather_json`, folded into plain numbers); a Workshop job stores its time, experience and Market Day mods in its input; a dungeon run stores the day in its input and summary; a raid stage's fight input carries the sheet, luck and attacks it was played with. A claim or a replay never reads the weather, so a day that changes (or leaves the content, or is forgotten) cannot change an old result. A planned or unknown id does nothing.
- **Market Day is limited.** It boosts the player's first craft of the game day (+5 pt one-step-up chance) and first 2 dismantle jobs (top of the range). Every job of that kind started that game day counts toward the limit, boosted or not (counted from `workshop_jobs.started_at` inside the day's bounds), so saving jobs for the day pays nothing.
- **Shipped (live): 9 of 10 Boons and 8 of 10 Omens.** Boons: Fair Winds, Warm Hearth, Clear Skies, High Water, Bonfire Night, Hearty Breakfast, Lantern Light, Market Day, Quiet Night. Omens: Heatwave, Static Sky, Heavy Rain, Long Shadows, Restless Wind, Cold Snap, Muddy Roads and the wild Stormfront.
- **Skipped, marked `planned` in the data, because the engine needs work first:** **Kind Rain** (every creature heals 2% of max HP at round start: there is no per-round heal on the sheet yet), **Morning Haze** (the first creature of each row skips its first turn: no skip-turn hook) and **Bramble Season** (the first reaction each round is reflected at 30%: a new reflect share in the reaction step). They are in the content with their words and a `planned` note, are never drawn, and the compendium lists them as not yet in the valley.
- **Forecast UI.** `GET /api/weather` answers today's and tomorrow's days (names, words, the numbers at the day's strength, start and end, wild). A wall calendar in the living room is the main place: a paper page that sways a little, comes off the wall on hover or keyboard focus, opens the forecast card on tap or Enter, and now and then says a short line in a speech bubble (a gap of 10 to 30 seconds, drawn on the client). The raid card and the dungeon pre-run card each get one compact line ("Sky today: Fair Winds and Heavy Rain.", or "Sky when Phase 2 runs" when the next stage is tomorrow). It is all text, prefers-reduced-motion is respected (the calendar keeps still), and the workbench has fixtures for the card, the line and the calendar.
- **Discord.** The raid reveal and stage posts get one sky line (escaped like every other name; `allowed_mentions: { parse: [] }` was already sent).
- **Analytics.** `weather_draw` (boon, omen, wild, rank, festival, strengths) when a day is drawn and `weather_use` (where: trip, job, dungeon, raid; boon, omen) when something is started or resolved under a day that changes it.
- **Staging tool.** The dev panel's `set-weather` sets a day's Boon and Omen by hand (staging only, like the other dev tools), so every card and effect can be looked at; it can set an Omen the draw would not.
- **Existing servers.** Migration 31 adds the `weather_days` table and `expeditions.weather_json`; nothing else changes. Trips sent before it have no weather and behave as before.
- **Sim.** The economy and the journey sims draw weather from a seeded stream of their own (`${seed}:weather`, drawn a day ahead at the rank of the moment) and apply the same pure functions; the raid sweep draws three days in a row for each week it plays (`weekSky`). It is on by default (`weather: false` or `--no-weather` for the run without it). A new study (`weather-study.ts`, `pnpm sim:weather`) plays raid weeks under each Boon and Omen alone against no weather on the same seeds, and the default journey server with and without weather. Push on and Claim carry no idempotency key (they spend nothing and the server stores the result); sending carries the key store's key, reused on a retry.
- **Claim reveal.** The failed state is its own red panel (what failed, with its odds, and "nothing came back"). Loot uses `RewardPile`, which now takes found supply ids (one icon per kind of supply, after the parts).
- **Journal and profile** are two buttons next to "Trips" on the Expeditions tab (no sixth tab). A page that is not found shows as a blank; its text is never in the page. Milestone names are client text (the rules carry ids only), with a fallback made from the id.
- **Slot wipe notice** is shown above the tabs once, the seen trip ids kept in `localStorage` behind try/catch (blocked storage means it shows again next session, which is harmless).
- **Workshop.** Supply crafting is a job kind: a Supplies section lists all twelve with effect, parts, hours and the Crafting level needed (the reason is written when none of your creatures has it), and uses the same creature picker; a claimed craft shows the supply.
- **Banners** are 64x24 pixel strips (`sprites/data/banner-sprites.ts`), one per destination, drawn from the briefs in `destinations.json` (every brief colour is in the banner and used; a test checks it), with no creature in them. They draw at a whole-number scale of 3 to 6 chosen from the space (`bannerScale`), so they stay sharp and never overflow a phone.
- **How to play** "Expeditions" says push-on, the Journal and the 24 h trip; the picture's trips come from `RULES.expeditions.lengths` (2 / 6 / 12 h) instead of the old v1 table.
- **Small-creature popover** is on the explorer walking on the banner, in the claim reveal and in the profile's explorer list; the picker's sprites are radio rows and the catalogue's residents are species (no level), so they stay plain.

**Layout check.** Headless Chromium at 390 px and 1024 px: no horizontal scroll on the catalogue, the send flow, running trips, the choice, the claim reveal (home, worked, failed), the journal, the profile and the Workshop's supplies; with `prefers-reduced-motion` the walker and bob have no animation.

## 2026-10-13: Expeditions v2 numbers set by simulation

ID: `2026-10-13-expeditions-v2-numbers-set-by-simulation` · Status: current, amended by `2026-10-13-the-slot-wipe-spares-finished-trips`, `2026-10-20-a-deeper-area-find-is-the`, `2026-10-20-expedition-trips-pay-in-ticks`, `2026-10-20-push-on-extra-time-starts-at`

Built from the spec above: the content, the server (migration 19, routes, Workshop supply jobs, analytics) and the simulator. The client UI is a separate task. Numbers live in `RULES.expeditions` (`packages/game/src/rules/config.ts`) and the content files in `packages/game/src/content/data` (`destinations.json`, `supplies.json`, `push-on-goals.json`, `journal.json`, `weekly-specials.json`); the full run is `docs/balance/report-2026-10-13-expeditions.md`. The v1 table `RULES.expedition` (singular) stays for trips that were running at launch and for the old client.

- **Trips.** 2 h, 6 h and 12 h, and 24 h from explorer rank 5. One catch roll for the first three, two for 24 h. A trip is free; only supplies cost Bait. Worth (the sim's measure) per trip is about 11 / 35 / 79 / 181 Bait for 2 / 6 / 12 / 24 h, so a longer trip pays more per hour than a shorter one. Explorer XP per trip: 4 / 110 / 300 / 700. A 12 h trip is the best Bait-free way to progress (2.6 times the best short strategy) and always sending 2 h trips is the worst (0.3 times the worst of the others); 99% of a daily player's trips are 12 or 24 h.
- **Ranks and slots.** XP for ranks 2 to 10: 200, 800, 2000, 4500, 10 000, 22 000, 45 000, 90 000, 160 000. Each rank above 1 makes that creature's trips 2% shorter. Slot 2 opens after 3 finished trips (day 1 for a daily player); slots 3, 4 and 5 open at a rank sum (the sum of every creature's rank above 1) of 26, 72 and 103: day 28, 121 and 370 for a daily player (targets: 30 plus or minus 7, about 120, about 365). The slot 5 threshold sits just under the most a daily player's creatures can reach (about 106), so a slot never gets out of reach.
- **Familiarity.** 1 star after 4 finished trips with that creature at that place, 2 for every species there caught, 3 for a shiny there. Stars open the deeper area (30 / 40 / 50% of rolls come from it) and a roll in the deeper area is one of that place's deep species 35% of the time. That is how Epic creatures are found (an Epic species lives only in a deeper area or at the Festival Grounds), so 4 trips at one place are the price of an Epic. Stars also give small rarity bonuses (2 / 4 / 8%) and 10 / 20% more parts at 2 / 3 stars.
- **Places and species.** Each species lives in 1 or 2 places; the Festival species only at the Festival Grounds (Festival weeks), where 12% of rolls are Epic. Tidepools have their own odds (24% Rare, so the Tide Rare Coralynx is not scarcer than the others), Embers and Verdant Hollow 4% Rare (Mossback lives in both, so it would be everywhere otherwise). Moonlit Reef is night-only (20:00 to 06:00 of the guild's own clock, daylight saving included, tested) and needs rank 4; the Old Crossroads need rank 2.
- **Supplies and parts.** Four kinds in three tiers (price in Bait 12 to 90). Trips bring few parts on purpose: 0 scrap on 2 h and 6 h trips, 1 on 12 h, 3 on 24 h, with shards, glimmer and blueprints rising with length. With more trips per day (two slots from day one) the v1 loot made the Crafting skill reach level 10 in 30 days against a target of 4 to 6, and that was the only thing that moved it: scrap from trips feeds crafting, so it was cut to the bone. Crafted supplies are a new Workshop job (Crafting level 1, 3 and 5 for the tiers, 2 to 6 h, scrap and shards).
- **Push on.** A trip offers a choice with 15% plus 1.5% per rank above 1 (a supply of the best tier always brings one; every supply adds its own chance). One safe and, from rank 3, one risky goal. (Changed 2026-10-20: the extra hours count from the choice.) A risky goal that fails makes the whole trip bring nothing. The odds of every risky goal were set so the expected worth equals coming home (between 0.96 and 1.00 in the sim; the target is within 10%): Shortcut 40 / 61 / 78%, Lost cache 45 / 66 / 82%, Press deeper 66 / 81%, Ancient vault 36 / 56% (after 6 h / 12 h / 24 h). Rare gear comes mostly from these goals.
- **Duplicates.** A duplicate of a creature at its 5 fusions becomes Bait at 60% of the usual amount on an expedition (`convertBp`). Trips are free now, so at the full amount a daily player's trips would pay for themselves and more; the amount was set by the sim so the Bait left for training matches what players had when trips cost Bait.
- **Account.** Ten milestones with bonuses of at most 2% (XP) or 1% (rarity, parts, choice odds). The stats are `trips_finished`, `trips_failed`, `trip_hours`, `species_found`, `places_visited`, `journal_pages`, `journal_sets`, `reactions_seen` (from raids), `pushons_taken`, `pushons_won`, `supplies_used` and `shinies_found`.
- **Slot wipe.** Migration 19 keeps each player's oldest running trip (by start time, then row order, so it is deterministic and replayable) and maps it to the Old Crossroads; the others are cancelled with no reward and their creatures freed. The Bait those trips cost is given back through the ledger (once, by trip id): the spec said "no reward", but charging Bait for a trip the player never got felt wrong, and it is a few dozen Bait at most. Everyone starts with 1 slot.
- **Randomness.** All live: catches from the existing catch rng, loot from the gear rng, and the choice offer, push-on result and journal from a new third stream (`tripRng`), so none of them can use another's draws in a test. Nothing is rolled before the moment it is needed, and every spend carries its key. `props_json` of analytics events is capped at 1536 characters now (1024 before): an `expedition_send` carries up to two supply ids.
- **Migration numbers.** Migration 18 is the daily dungeon's and 19 is this one (the branch first shipped an empty step 18 as a placeholder; the merge put the dungeon's migration there).

How the numbers were found (and what the sim taught us):

- The raid targets move when trips become free. Each raid week's boss rank is chosen from the roster's average creature level, so players who spend Bait differently end up at another level and meet a different boss: half a level more or less flips the rank, and the win rate with it. The sim therefore matches what players spend on training before anything else: supplies are bought from the Bait above a small reserve (casual and daily players alike, a convex rule, which copies how v1 spending rose with income) and trips are sent before training. The creature levels at weeks 4 and 8 are within half a level of v1.
- Epic creatures matter for raid power; with only a 1% base Epic chance and no deeper-area shortcut the sim's rosters lacked them and the 15-player finale fell to 10%. The deeper-area rule (a deep species 35% of deep rolls) fixed that.
- The result (seed `leet`, full run, 43 targets): 42 pass. Typical finale 49.9% (40 to 60), Phase 2 75.0%, 15 players 36.4% (35 to 65), boss spread 2.0 points, hard pity 13.3%, gear share at week 8 12.3% (10 to 20), Essence held at week 12 580 below today's and falling, Crafting level at day 30 6 (4 to 6), casual species never more than 0.1 above today's curve (limit 0.3).
- **One target is not met: setup credit share, 14.1% (needs 15%).** It was 15.2% before expeditions v2 (the old note says it passed by 0.2), so a small shift in what rosters hold is enough to lose it. Rosters have a few more setters (Epic Solmane) and Festival creatures than before; about twenty variants (place odds, XP, supply policy, Bait conversion, affinity) moved it between 13.5% and 14.7% but never to 15%. Lowering the target to 14% is a design call; it is left as it is.

## 2026-10-13: Expeditions v2 spec (Misa)

ID: `2026-10-13-expeditions-v2-spec` · Status: current, amended by `2026-10-16-supplies-cannot-be-bought-the-dungeon` · Amends: `2026-10-10-game-rules-from-the-research-review`

Reviewed in a shared doc. Expeditions become creature-centred: an explorer rank and per-destination familiarity per creature, with account-wide stats and only very small account bonuses. Slots start at 1, the 2nd comes on day one, then about a month, four months and a year, from the sum of creature explorer ranks; at launch, trips in slots 2–3 are cancelled with no reward. Trips without supplies are free. Supplies (bought with Bait, crafted or found) give a meaningful boost, and the best ones guarantee an end-of-trip push-on choice with clearly flagged risk. The minimum trip is 2 h, and rewards grow faster than the hours. There is a pre-written Journal that hints at mechanics, banner cards instead of a map, and a weekly special. Raids stay the main source of big loot. Targeted trips are deferred. Full spec in `design/expeditions-v2.md`.
