# UX patterns: how Leet Creatures screens are built

The standing rules for the Activity's UI, learned from Misa's play-test feedback (October 2026). Read this before any UI work, together with `decisions.md` (the reasoning and history behind each rule). When a rule here and a screen disagree, the screen is the bug. When a new pattern is settled, add it here in a line or two and link its decision.

Everything is checked at 390 px wide first (Discord on a phone), in the component workbench (`docs/design/workbench.md`).

## Layers: drawers, sheets and popovers

- **One drawer at a time.** A drawer never opens another drawer or sheet. A choice inside a drawer opens _inside the same drawer_ as a drill-in view with "‹ Back" at the top left (Back or Escape returns without a change). Example: "Equip on" in the Workshop item drawer, and the dungeon camp's three steps (keepsake, who goes home, arrange the row) inside the camp's card. The top of every drill-in is `ui/DrillHead` (Back, then the title; the view around it handles Escape with `onDrillKey`); never put Back at the bottom. A sheet may open from a page (the raid and dungeon creature picker), never from a drawer.
- **Where a creature list opens.** One rule for `ui/CreatureList`. From a page or a small control: the shared sheet `ui/RosterPicker` (`pick` mode). Inside a drawer: a drill-in view of the same drawer, with `ui/DrillHead` (`pick` mode, `flat`). A big page-level decision (send a trip, start a job) may show the list inline in the page (`choose` mode). Never a sheet over a drawer.
- **What a creature list shows.** Every row always shows the basics: the creature's picture, its name in its rarity colour, and its level (quiet grey). Beyond that, show only what the player needs to make an informed choice for the activity the creature is picked for: the bare minimum, not everything we know (for example crafting level and job time for a job; explorer rank, familiarity, element and role for an expedition; the worn item for Equip on). What that is differs per activity and is not always clear, so a new use of the list decides it case by case and raises it with Misa when unsure. Not set in stone; always consider it when using the list. (Misa, 2026-10-21)
- **Drawers have no X.** A tap outside, the handle (tap or drag down) or Escape closes them; the close button stays for the keyboard and screen readers and shows only when focused. A _panel inside_ a drawer that has no other visible way to close may have a small X (the item picker's detail panel).
- **Small help popovers** (glossary tags) may float over anything. A creature's popover starts like its card: the name in its rarity's colour, then its tags (rarity, element, role, Shiny, First on server when its owner holds the record (the server says so for guild-mates' loungers), and on your own lounger in the room its "Lounging 34 / 60 XP" or "Lounging · Rested", as on its card; each opens its explanation inside the popover), then its facts as a `DataTable` in the pixel font, as on the creature page (Misa, 2026-10-20: the body font looked wrong there; sentences in a popover stay in the body font). Its buttons ("Move", "Swap") sit side by side under its facts; one closes the popover before it acts, so "Swap" opening the lounge swap sheet is a sheet from the page, not from a popover.
- **The living room's colour is `--lounge`** (tokens.css; today the same green as `--good`): the "Lounging" tag (filled) and "Rested" (outlined). Use the token, not `--good`, for anything about lounging.
- If a flow starts to need layers on layers, stop and redesign it (drill-in, inline expansion), don't stack.

## The frame: navigation, pages, the HUD and Home

How the game's screens hang together (decisions.md 2026-10-24, the navigation, Home, shell and Collection entries). A new screen or feature fits into this frame; it never adds a second navigation, a footer or a bar of its own.

- **Places come from one list.** A screen the player can go to is a place: one `TABS` entry with its group (`tabs.ts`); `navPlaces` turns it into a button with its pip, and the dock, the trays and the rail follow. Never add a navigation button by hand. Groups are `NAV_GROUPS` (Collection: Creatures, Workshop, Journal; Adventure: Expeditions, Dungeon, Raid, Games); a group with one place is a direct button. **Home** (`NAV_HOME`) is in no group: the app opens on it.
- **Phone: a dock; desktop: a rail** (`MainNav`). On a phone the dock is three buttons at the very bottom (a group, Home, a group), each a small picture over its word; Home is the only gold, raised button and carries no pip. A group opens an upward tray of its places (the current one gold, a pip reason per place). From 45 rem (720 px) to 60 rem the bottom is **one bar with every place in a row** (`bar`, `BAR_QUERY` in `ui/wide-layout.ts`; Home first, gold but flat; no trays): it starts where the longest word fits its share with room to breathe, so each added place pushes the breakpoint up by about 5 rem. From 60 rem the rail lists Home and every place under its group's heading. The dock's background reaches the screen's bottom edge, with at most 0.5 rem of the safe-area inset as padding inside it; anything fixed to the bottom on a phone (the staging DEV button) sits above it.
- **Pages and Back.** A place opened from the dock or rail has no Back: the navigation is on screen and says where you are. A page opened from another page (Settings; Home has no page of its own and no page pins) is a drill-in: `ui/DrillHead` with "‹ Back" to where it came from, and Escape (`onDrillKey`). Every change of place or page lands at the top of the new page (`use-scroll-top.ts`); never keep the old scroll position.
- **The HUD is the only bar** (`Hud`). On a phone it is fixed to the top of the window, full width, the same at every scroll position (never sticky), reaching up behind Discord's own bar; the How to play page's head does the same. On a wide window it is the card's top row. It is slim (2.75rem): its buttons are drawn 2.25rem high and an invisible strip makes each a 44 px target. **Buttons that sit still while the page scrolls (the dock, its trays, the HUD's cog) act on a finger's touch down** (`ui/instant-press.ts`), so a tap during a momentum scroll is never lost; never use it on something inside the scrolling page. It holds the place's name (the page's one `h1`), the currencies and one button, the Settings cog. Things about the whole game live behind it: the currency popover holds the balances, the recent Bait income and a failing leet sync; Settings holds the device's display options and How to play. There is no footer. Build facts (branch, commit, environment) are `<meta name="build-*">` tags and the staging DEV panel's Build section (`build-info.ts`), never visible to players.
- **Settings are display preferences on the device** (`settings/SettingsPage`, `home/board-settings.ts`): one typed object, read and written inside try/catch, each field falling back to its default; never game rules or anything the server should know. The one exception is the Discord DMs section (`settings/DiscordDms.tsx`, the server's per-player opt-ins, loaded when the page opens and saved on each change; see `dm-notifications.md`). A new option is one `SettingRow` (name, a hint line, a control from `ui/`: `Segmented`, `Switch`) in a section.
- **Home is where a feature talks to the player** (`home/HomePage`, `home/NoticeBoard`). The living room sits on top (on a phone; on a wide window it is the band under the HUD), then the Today board. A feature that needs the player adds a **notice row** (an id and a reason in `home-view.ts` / `home/board.ts`: "Needs you" for waiting work, "Coming up" for a time, "Quiet places" when idle notices are shown), not a widget of its own. A long-lived record of the player's (the Journal, Stats) is a **place of the Collection group**, not a pin on the board (Misa, 2026-10-25: pins did not fit under Needs you, and their late-loading teasers made the layout jump). The board has two round **corner pins** at the top right: the "?" (the rightmost) opens How to play, the starter's (to its left) opens Creatures; they share the `hb-badge` look. The board's tilt option is `none` (flush, the default) or `still`; there is no motion. The board's text is one step smaller than a page's. **Everything on the board that is about another screen opens it** (Misa, 2026-10-25): a Needs-you notice, a Coming up row, a Quiet places row and the Games medals go to their place through the one `onGo`; the sky line opens the forecast card (the room calendar's popover). Keep 44 px targets and no nested buttons. The room's presence and XP run from the game screen (`use-lounge-presence.ts`) whether or not the room is drawn. **A card on Home never fills in text after the page has drawn** (Misa, 2026-10-25: the Journal and Stats teasers made the board jump): show only what the page already holds, or reserve the line, or leave it out.
- **Adding a feature, in short:** a place in `TABS` if it is a screen; a notice row if it ever needs the player; a row in `stats-view.ts` for each number worth keeping; a `SettingRow` only for a display preference; a workbench entry for each new component. Check the dock, the tray and Home at 360 and 1100 px.

## Layout

- **Equal edges.** Every block in a drawer or card shares the same left and right edges (`--drawer-pad`). Pixel rings are drawn outside their box (3 px on buttons, 2 px on accordions): sit ringed elements in by the ring so they line up with text and cards.
- **Never clip a border.** A scroll or clip container (swipe area, scroll box) gets a few pixels of inline padding with a matching negative margin, so frames and rings at the edge show whole.
- **Order of a detail view:** what it is (the card), what you do with it (Equip on, …), Details, then the **destructive action last, centred, apart from the rest** (Dismantle).
- **The creature card** (`CreatureCard`, the Creatures list; Misa's variant E, decisions.md 2026-10-19): the sprite at the top left; beside it the name on one line (never wrapped; a name too wide ends in "…") and, under the name, the creature's tags in words (the species of a nickname, the element with its icon, the role, Festival, Shiny, First on server, Away, On a job, "Lounging 34 / 60 XP", Rested), and at the end of the name's line, only for a free creature that is not the lounger, a quiet armchair (icon only, the page's colour until hovered or focused) that sends it to the living room (2026-10-20); then the slim experience bar across the whole card (`ui/XpTrack`: the level tag at its left, one width from "Lv 1" to "Lv 30" so the bars of a list line up, the numbers inside); then the gear slots on the left and the talents on the right, **one to a row** (each talent, each empty slot and the closed slots' "20 · 25" on a row of their own, even when two would fit on one); then the gold button when something is ready. No level tag in a corner and no arrow: the whole card is one tap target that opens the page (the name is the button for the keyboard and screen readers); the gear slots and the talents open the page on their part; Away, On a job and the level keep their tap-for-help. Every ringed thing (level tag, gear rings, the gold button) sits in by its ring, so all edges line up with the sprite. The creature page starts the same way (`creature-page/PageHeader` reuses `CreatureHead` and `XpTrack`): sprite, name, tags, then the level and bar row, then the full-width action buttons.
- **A long panel scrolls inside itself**, capped so the context above stays visible (at least two grid rows). No scrollbar: use `ui/ScrollHint` (a slow drift after 2.5 s that stops on any touch; a fade under reduced motion).

- **Nothing overflows its parent** (Misa, 2026-10-23). No breakouts of the card's padding: a scene, a map or a show fits its parent's content width (a pixel scene scales to it, at most its cap, centred). Names cut with "…", rows of chips wrap, flex and grid children get `min-width: 0`. Check at 320, 360, 390, 412, 768 and 1100 px.
- **A main area with a side panel** (`ui/WideLayout`, `ui/SidePanel`): from 60 rem (`WIDE_QUERY`) the app card widens and the panel sits beside the main area; below it the panel's content goes under the main area or into a sheet. The Expeditions world map (the place panel, with an X) and the Valley Games show (the leaderboard, titled with the event, no X) use it. See decisions.md, 2026-10-23.
- **A map's markers are buttons.** On the Expeditions world map every place is a real button over a decorative canvas (spoken label: name, who is there, time rule; arrow keys move along it). Choosing a place runs the same flow (`PlaceFlow`: the place, then the send wizard and the push-on choice as drill-ins) in a sheet on a phone (`PlaceSheet`) and in a side panel on a desktop (`PlacePanel`). On a desktop the map is full width until a place is chosen; the panel then opens beside it with an X to close it, and the map glides so the place stays in its middle. The panel never takes focus by itself and a live region names the place. A map that pans can be dragged with a mouse (`ui/use-drag-scroll`). See decisions.md, 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-22.

## Text

- **Less text.** Drop anything the screen already says (no "Needs level 12" next to an icon that reads "Lv 12"; no "Next: …" lines). Requirements and refusal reasons stay, short.
- **Numbers and resources are badges with pictures** (`ui/CountBadge`: parts, shards, Essence, ranges like "3–4"), never prose like "Gives 3 to 4 scrap…". A duration is a badge with the clock glyph.
- **Terms players meet get a tap-for-help tag** (a glossary entry, one short line). **Tags never draw a "?"** (Misa, 2026-10-20): a word gets a dotted underline, a chip or tag shows nothing extra and brightens on hover; the only "?" is the small help button next to a heading or a control (`look="help"`). Player-facing names are plain words: "blueprint quality", not "well-thumbed marks". Code names may stay; only what players read changes.
- **Counts read "3 / 12"**, never "3 of 12".
- **Result screens read alike.** Every XP gain has one look everywhere (a Workshop job, a trip, each dungeon team member; creature experience, explorer XP and Crafting XP alike): the shared `ui/XpGain` row ("Count-up", decisions.md 2026-10-19). The label and the level change with "Level up!", then the level tag, a slim bar that counts up from the old value to the new one, the "+N" and the multiplier: a "3x" chip (the game's plain small tag, bone on ink, never the kind's colour, no ring, no icon beside it) when the total is not 1x; it opens the breakdown popover (each source as its multiplier, "Treat 3x", "Hearty Breakfast 1.25x", "Cold Snap 0.8x", the treat's line led by the treat icon, then "Total 3x" in gold). **Multipliers read "2x", "1.25x", "0.8x"** everywhere, chip and breakdown alike: a decimal with an "x", up to two decimals, no trailing zeros, never "×2" or "+25%". A popover's lines are its body text (the body font in bone, as in a glossary popover), also when they are a `dl`. "Level up!" and "Rank up!" rise into place from below in pixel steps with a small overshoot; with reduced motion they are simply there. **No prose about a gain**: never "Treat gave 2x experience", a "Treat" word tag or a line naming the day's Boons and Omens; the breakdown says it. A room-by-room recap stamps only fights (CLEARED or DEFEATED); a camp, cache, mystery room or door has no stamp and says what happened ("Rested", "Visited"). The stamp has a fixed maximum width, so it stays a stamp on a computer screen. A panel's header is two short lines that never wrap to three at 390 px. Never print a timezone ID: say the time and the countdown ("Resets at Wed 13:37, in 2 d 3 h").
- **Anything gained or found uses the Find components, so a player learns to recognise a reward.** Resources (Essence, Bait, parts, supplies) are count badges (`ui/CountBadge`, tappable for help, like every tag without a "?"). Gear is a `ui/FindItem` (given its inventory `id` it is inspectable: a tap, or hover with a mouse, opens the full `ItemCard` in a popover, with a centred, content-width Equip button under it that opens the creature sheet of the Workshop's "Equip on" list (`EquipHost`); without an id it is plain), a blueprint a `ui/FindBlueprint` (the same box: the item's picture in a plain dashed frame, "Blueprint: Name", a Blueprint help tag, the tags the crafted item will have, never a rarity), a creature a `ui/FindCreature` (a trip's `FindCard` adds the duplicate choice under it). They sit in a `ui/FindList`: boxes side by side, centred, wrapping with a gap; a creature's card takes a whole line. One section holds everything gained (no sub-headings by source); a separate bonus (the dungeon's streak) is its own titled section with the same components. Never write a find as a sentence ("You won Lanternfin!") or draw a second layout for one. A screen that is not a find (a worn item, a cost, the item card) may reuse a row but is not a reward.

## Fonts and sizes

- **The pixel font is set per panel, not per element.** Game panels carry `.pix-panel` (everything inside inherits Jersey 10); long reading text opts out with `.prose`. Never rely on adding `.pix` to each new line: that is how wrong fonts kept slipping in. A reveal card is made a pixel panel with `RevealShell`'s `pix` option (the dungeon's camp, mystery room and descent cards use it): its sentences are `.prose`, everything else inherits the pixel font. A `ChoiceList` always sits in such a panel.
- **Sizes come from the type scale** in `styles/base/tokens.css`: `--fs-title` 1.25 rem (titles, the creature's name), `--fs-heading` 1 rem (item and sheet names, card headings), `--fs-row` 0.875 rem (rows, lines, options, panel buttons), `--fs-small` 0.75 rem (notes, badges, reasons, small labels, the navigation's words). Every text size in the app is one of these four since 2026-10-19 (and one rung smaller than the first scale since the same day, Misa: "smaller everywhere", for a 360 px phone); there are no raw `font-size` values in `styles/`. The only raw sizes left are display and art (the page's base size and h1, the pixel heading, the living room's emoji, the How to play illustrations, the stage's effects) and the compact labels of the 9-slot boards and the stage's unit names, which must be smaller than `--fs-small` to fit. `type-scale.test.ts` lists them file by file and fails on a new one. Don't add raw `font-size` values. Check a type change at 360 px as well as 390 px: nothing may clip or spill (the navigation's longest words, "Adventure" in a dock third and "Expeditions" in the tray and the rail, are the tightest fits).
- Run the font audit after UI changes: `CHROME=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194/chrome-linux/chrome node scripts/font-audit.mjs` in `apps/activity` (add the new presets to it).

## Choosing things

- **Pick lists show everyone.** Who can do it comes first; who can't is listed after, greyed, with a short reason on the right of the row ("Needs level 7", "Guards only", "Away on a trip", "On a job"). No separate "who cannot" section. Pass the server's actual refusal rules through as reasons, so a tap never fails afterwards. **One exception (Misa, 2026-10-21):** in the Workshop's job pickers (`JobDrawer`, step Who crafts) the creatures under the job's Crafting level fold into one greyed summary row ("4 more creatures need Crafting level 3") that a tap expands, greyed with "Needs level 3" on each; busy creatures at the level stay listed with their reason, and when nobody can, the footer says why.
- **A switch the player can't afford still takes the tap** and says why in place, for a few seconds ("Not enough Bait" in the treat row's second line, with a small shake), instead of a greyed switch with a reason line outside it (Misa, 2026-10-20). Packed or on is shown by the switch and a lit icon, not a coloured edge.
- **A hint about who suits a choice is one binary badge, never a score or a ranking** (the Games' green "Good fit" tick, `GoodFitMark`; Misa, 2026-10-23). The list puts the badged ones first, then the rest by name, and the screen's help button explains the badge. See decisions.md, 2026-10-23.
- **Select many** (decisions.md 2026-10-27): a button on the page starts a selection mode (`DismantleSelect`: `SelectTools`, `SelectBar`). A tap on a tile toggles its mark (shown at once; `ItemTile` `marked`: red corner, dimmed art, spoken in its label), a small separate "i" on each tile (`onInfo`) opens the item's card so a phone can still read stats, and the card always has the same action. Presets only mark what matches; the player toggles single items after. The bar sticks inside its section (above the dock), counts, and leads to the usual wizard in a drawer.
- **A pick acts right away** (no extra confirm button), unless the action is destructive or costs something; then one clear button with the cost.
- **A trigger that opens a list looks like a dropdown** (current choice with sprite and name, a chevron), not a native `<select>`.
- **A tap shows, a drag moves.** Tapping a creature on a board opens what it is (its popover or configurator) and never moves it; moving is by dragging (the camp lost tap-to-swap, decisions.md 2026-10-21).
- **Grids open with nothing selected.** Tapping an item shows its details; tapping it again (or the details' X) closes them.
- **A choice with one option is still shown.** Evolving always opens the evolve screen; with one form it shows that form on one card, already picked, with the same button, cost and "permanent" line and no sentence about there being one (show, don't tell).
- **Keep the player anchored.** The item or creature shown last stays marked (light edge inside the rarity edge) and in view after its details close; going back to a list restores where it was and marks the creature viewed last.

## Shared components (use these, don't rebuild them)

**Before building a screen or a part of one, find the existing component or layout that does the same job and reuse it** (this table, then the workbench). A new screen that needs "a main area with a panel beside it", "a list to pick a creature from" or "a drawer with a wizard" uses the one that exists, extended if needed, never a look-alike. If nothing fits, say so in the brief or the PR. (Retro 2026-10-23: the Games show got its own layout until Misa asked for the expedition map's.)
