# The boss track and the roster seam

How the lab plans which raid boss a server meets at which Boss Rank, and how it tunes those bosses without knowing yet how fast servers grow. Misa, 2026-10-30: combat v2's role is to set the challenges and make them easy to configure, then find the expected path for a server (first boss, next boss, and so on) and tune for it. Another branch is redesigning the passive systems at the same time (the valley: posts, scouts, the Forge, catch-up; `docs/design/valley/` on its branch), so the speed at which rosters grow is unknown. Work here must not be wasted when the two systems meet.

## The split

| Part                                                            | Owner      | Where                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The challenges: which boss at which rank, its cards and numbers | combat v2  | `packages/lab/src/track.ts` (the track), `bosses.ts` (the bosses), the tuner              |
| The targets: what a server should achieve against them          | combat v2  | `packages/lab/src/targets.ts` (Misa's targets, 2026-10-30)                                |
| The roster: what a server's players have at week N              | the valley | `packages/lab/data/valley/` (the valley's files), read by `src/valley.ts` and `roster.ts` |
| The path: where a server is at week N, and how long it stays    | both meet  | `packages/lab/src/path.ts`: the track, the targets and the roster run together            |

The roster is the only thing the valley changes. When the valley has new numbers it copies them over its two files in `packages/lab/data/valley/`, and the tuner reruns (about 4 minutes); the track, the bosses, the targets and the path code stay.

## The seam: a roster profile

A roster profile says, for each kind of player and each week from 1 to 24:

- **Levels:** of the player's 1st, 3rd, 10th and 20th creature (the four the valley's economy lab plans to report; levels between are interpolated). The level cap is 30.
- **Roster size:** how many creatures the player has, and which lines (lab forms) a new server can have (starters plus what scouts find).
- **Talents:** how many talent slots are filled (slot 2 at level 20, slot 3 at 25 today) and whether they are picked or random.
- **Gear:** slots that can be worn and the item level. The lab has no gear yet (the gear plan is a later step), so this field is carried but unused until then.

Kinds of player: **daily** (the valley's "Sanni"), **weekly** (about 75% to 80% of a daily player's idle growth, valley target T-2) and **new** (joins late and catches up).

**The data is the valley's** (2026-10-30): `packages/lab/data/valley/roster-profile.json` (exactly the profile above) and `server-coverage.json` (see "Ownability"), copied from the valley branch `ccr-93a1de17-yutb1a`, commit `fb0b3c3`, `docs/design/valley/economy/`. `src/valley.ts` loads them, checks their shape (24 weeks per kind, numbers in range, `forms` is `'all'` or a list (the valley now lists the held forms by name; nothing in the lab reads them, every form is allowed), the coverage's `unit` is `"line"`) and throws a clear error otherwise, so a bad drop-in fails loudly. Some values are fractions (a 10th creature at level 18.5). **A level of 0 means no such creature** (the median player has not reached that rank yet: a daily player's 20th is 0 through week 8, a weekly player's through week 12); only the 1st is at least 1. `levelOfCreature` skips a 0 and never interpolates toward it: a creature ranked past the last non-zero anchor takes that anchor's level (the old flat-beyond-the-20th rule, applied at the last rank that exists), and the size still limits how many creatures there are. The valley's new player is not clamped to the daily player's; `rosterWeek` caps their levels and size at the daily player's of the same week. `gear` is the average worn item level and is still unused.

The three **speeds** stay: **slow** (levels 20% behind), **expected**, **fast** (20% ahead). They are sensitivity factors on the valley's levels, not data of the valley: they show how the path moves if servers grow slower or faster than the valley says.

## The evolution gate

A creature fights as the form it has. A stage 2 form evolves at the level the stage 1 form's `evolvesTo` says (15 for every line today), and a daily player's best creature is level 11 in week 1, 14 in week 2 and 17 in week 3. So a creature of a line whose roster level is under its form's evolution level fights as its **stage 1 form**: the Knack stays if the stage 1 form can equip it (or there is none), else it is the stage 1 form's first Knack, and a talent stays only if the stage 1 form can carry it. At or above the evolution level, and for any stage 1 form, nothing changes (`gatedPick`, `levelledColumn` in `roster.ts`; `gate: false` leaves a line as written).

It applies wherever a line is levelled for the track: the dedicated server, the classic party, the mixed server, the slot's own line (`SLOT_LINE`) and the boss answers in the tuner. A week-1 team is therefore a team of stage 1 forms, and slot 1's numbers are tuned for it. Before the gate the lab fielded stage 2 forms from week 1, and slot 1 came out near 12,400 HP, several times too much for the creatures a new server has.

**Slots 1 and 2 name stage 1 lines** (`SLOT_LINE`, the lead, 2026-10-31). A week-1 or week-2 server has no evolved forms, so the line the tuner measures as prepared there is the line such a server really fields: the gated version of the line it had (each stage 2 form its stage 1 form, with the Knack and the talents the gate keeps). Slot 1: the old commons line gated (Ashmole, Mudlump, Mistnewt, Splashet, Zipmouse, Sproutle); slot 2: the Cinder Wyrm's first answer gated, which keeps its tool, Squelch Song, on three Squelch (the first card's answer; the tool is worth 22 points of potential against the 10 asked). A team search over the common stage 1 forms found lines with more raw potential at the same numbers (slot 1: 277% against 132%; slot 2: 177% against 132% with the tool kept), but retuned they were worse where the tuner looks (slot 1 at the lower limit again with 180% and 115% with random talents; slot 2 at 4,800 HP with 107% with random talents), so the gated lines are kept. With them slot 1 is tuned to 3,800 HP / 83 Attack / 19 armour (the HP at the search's lower limit, with every target met) and slot 2 to 7,500 / 80 / 42. The ownability test's evolved half passes for every slot.

## The track

The track is data: an ordered list of slots, each a boss and the strength of its cards. The order of the bosses is one line (`TRACK_ORDER`): Tidal Colossus, Cinder Wyrm, Storm Sovereign, Old Bogback, Thornmother, and then the lap repeats, so Thornmother is slots 5, 10 and 15 (Misa, 2026-10-30; she used to be the third boss, by the shipped game's order). Her first appearance, slot 5, is a **wall** (below). A server starts at slot 1; a win moves it to the next slot (the Boss Rank rises by one); a failed boss returns the next week at full HP with last week's best on its bar (Misa, 2026-10-30). After the last boss the track starts again with stronger cards. A young server ramps in (below, "Tuning": slot 1 has no rule cards, slot 2 one). The first order is the shipped game's boss order; it is one line to change.

## The path

`path.ts` plays a server week by week: the roster at that week fights the boss of its current slot over a weekend; a kill moves it on, a loss keeps it. Festival weeks (every fifth week) pause the track: no raid boss that week, and afterwards the server continues where it was, with no rank ease (Misa, 2026-10-30). **The Festival clock is the server's age in weeks**, counted from week 1, the server's first raid week: weeks 5, 10, 15 and 20 fight nothing, whatever slot the server is on. It reports, per kind of server (dedicated, mixed, builders) and per roster speed:

- the week a server first fights each slot, and the weekends it spends there. **The arrival week is the first week the slot is fought**, never a Festival week: a server that wins slot 4 in week 4 has slot 5 in the Festival week and fights it first in week 6. The tuner and `SLOT_WEEKS` use this week;
- the longest run on one boss, and the share of servers stuck more than 4 weeks (the shipped balance target allows at most 10%).

The **mixed server** (`MIXED`) is ten players: two min-maxers and three builders (daily players) and five casual players (weekly players). Its min-maxers and builders field the written answer chosen for the slot (`SLOT_MIXED_ANSWER`, see "Tuning"); the casual players bring their best creatures with no Knack.

The **builder server** (`BUILDERS`) is four daily players who field the slot's prepared line with random legal talents instead of the written ones. It is information: it shows whether a wall such as slot 5 stops anyone who has the line's forms and Knacks but not its talents. `--path` prints it beside the other two; the tuner never plays it.

The path plays with Determination, which is on by default from the second lap (see "Tuning"). `--path --determination` also prints the path without it, beside it; `--path --no-determination` prints only the path without it.

## Tuning

Each slot is tuned for the roster a dedicated server has in the week it usually first fights it (the path says when), at the expected roster speed only: a slow server arrives later and keeps growing while a failed boss returns, so the path at three speeds, not a measure at the expected arrival week, is where the speed's effect shows. Path and tuning repeat until the arrival weeks equal the weeks the table was tuned for (`node scripts/report.mjs --tune --track [--slots 1-15] [--quick]`, `trackTuner.ts`, about 2.5 minutes on 4 cores for 15 slots). A tuning is a sweep up the track, because a slot's arrival depends only on the slots before it; one sweep settles it from an empty table. The result is the `SLOT_NUMBERS` table in `track.ts`.

**The main measure is damage potential** (Misa, 2026-10-30; `docs/design/decisions/raids.md`): the lab plays the whole raid weekend as if the boss could not die (`FightOptions.potential`: the boss stays at 1 HP, every stage and round is played, knockouts, attrition between stages and Build-up run as normal, a Culling Strike does not end it) and adds up all the damage as a share of the boss's HP (`weekendPotentialOf`, mean and spread; 1.4 is 140%). The kills, the HP removed and the retreat tiers are still measured with the normal fight. The targets, in `targets.ts`:

- **Prepared** (the boss's first written answer, or the slot's own line where `SLOT_LINE` names one (slots 1 and 2; see "The evolution gate"), with its written talents: the solved puzzle): potential **125% or more** (`PREPARED_POTENTIAL_MIN`). The same line with random legal talents is measured and reported, with no target.
- **Classic** (the unprepared but decent trio): potential **70% to 90%** (`CLASSIC_POTENTIAL_MIN`, `CLASSIC_POTENTIAL_MAX`) and, as before, a kill in at most 5% of weekends.
- Kept: `prepared.beat2` (a kill within two weekends, 75% or more) and the prepared line's HP removed with random talents (80% or more).
- **The tool's worth is a target in points of potential** (the lead's call, 2026-10-30; `toolPotentialMinAt` in `targets.ts`): the slot's tool, taken out of the prepared line (written talents), costs at least **5 points of potential at 0% card strength, 10 at 30%, rising straight to 35 at 100%** (flat above). A card asks at low strength and requires at full: without its tool a team at 130% falls under 100%. The tuner's weight for it is 2, under the potentials' 4. The tool's worth in points of the HP share (`tool.share`, `tool.written`) is **information only** on the track: it cannot hold once a prepared team reaches 125%, since such a team removes nearly all the HP with its tool or without it. The fixed ranks' test rules (`raidRules.ts`) still hold the HP-share tool targets (6 to 10 points).
- **The wall** (Misa, 2026-10-30, "Thornmother is a deliberate wall at the end of the first lap" in `docs/design/decisions/raids.md`): a per-slot flag in `track.ts` (`SLOT_WALL`, slot 5 only; `TrackSlot.wall`). Her bark makes a team with her answer far stronger than one without, and at the end of the first lap a server has a roster to rethink. On a wall slot `slotTargetsFor` asks the same as on any slot (prepared potential 125% or more, `prepared.beat2`, the tool's worth, the classic kill at most 5%) except the classic party's potential: **30% to 60%** (`WALL_CLASSIC_POTENTIAL_MIN`, `WALL_CLASSIC_POTENTIAL_MAX`) instead of 70% to 90%, and the tuner aims at 45% there (`WALL_CLASSIC_POTENTIAL_AIM`; the prepared aim stays 130%). Her later laps, slots 10 and 15, are normal slots: a server that passed the wall has learned the answer.
- Among numbers that meet every target the tuner prefers the prepared potential nearest 130% and the classic potential nearest 80% (`PREPARED_POTENTIAL_AIM`, `CLASSIC_POTENTIAL_AIM`, the lead's call), and then the numbers nearest the old ranks' line. The first-weekend kill aim it replaced (`PREPARED_KILL_AIM`) is gone.
- **Mixed servers are tuned too** (Misa, 2026-10-30, "if possible"): on every slot but the tutorial, a mixed server (ten players, five of them casual, the path's mapping, expected speed) needs at most **3 weekends**. It is measured as the mixed server's kill rate per weekend, read as the chance of a kill within three weekends (`1 - (1 - p)^3`, the way `prepared.beat2` reads the prepared line): half of the servers or more, which is a kill rate of about 21% or more (`MIXED_KILL_MIN`). The weight is 0.5, below every dedicated target's, so where both cannot hold together the dedicated ones win and the tuner reports the miss. Slot 1's 80% first-weekend kill is the tutorial's own, stronger version and replaces it there. The mixed server's potential and HP share are shown beside its kill rate and the expected weekends to a kill (`1 / p`). The fixed-rank rules (`raidRules.ts`) stay as they were and are not applied to slots.
- **The mixed server fields the written answer that suits it** (design panel 6, the lead, 2026-10-31). The first answer wants a Wide Reach Guard in every column, and every casual column of a mixed server leaves a gap; the boss's second answer covers whole rows instead (Row Ward), which a builder per row could carry. So the mixed server's min-maxers and builders field whichever of the boss's written answers (`BOSS_ANSWERS`, the first being the slot's own line where `SLOT_LINE` names one: `slotAnswersOf`) gives the mixed server the best kill rate at the slot. It is chosen once per slot, at the slot's numbers and arrival week, in the tuner's full measurement: every written answer is played by the mixed server on the same casual rosters and seeds (`measureSpeed`), and the best is kept, the target measure being its kill rate. Two guards: only an answer a mixed server of 8 can own at the slot's week is a candidate (`lineOwnable`, the same 50% rule as the tool line; the first answer always is), and another answer replaces the first only when it kills more often by 5 points (`MIXED_ANSWER_MARGIN`; a measurement of 40 draws is noisy, and the table should not flip on luck). The choice is data, `SLOT_MIXED_ANSWER` in `track.ts` (an index into the boss's answers; absent is 0, the first), which the path reads (`serverWeekend`: only the mixed server uses it, the dedicated and the builder server keep the slot's prepared line) and the tuner prints for pasting. It holds **the second answer at slots 6 and 11** (the Tidal Colossus with Breakwater, where whole-row wards carry the casual columns); at every other slot the first answer is as good or better (Results, below).

**The young-server ramp** is a column of the track (`SLOT_CARDS` in `track.ts`; `slotBoss` builds from it). Slot 1 is a **tutorial** boss: no rule cards and no Build-up, only its plain attack and its moves (the moves stay: they are the boss's attacks, not rules, and card strength does not scale them); with no card there is nothing to answer, so slot 1 has no tool target, and a new server has only its starters, so no classic limit either: its targets are the prepared potential, `prepared.beat2` and the HP removed with random talents. **The tutorial is a tutorial for every server** (design panel 6, 2026-10-30): slot 1 has one more target, `mixed.kill`, in `slotTargetsFor` and nowhere else: the mixed server (the path's mixed mapping, ten players, five of them casual) kills it in its first weekend in at least 80% of weekends (`TUTORIAL_MIXED_KILL_MIN`, weight 2). The prepared potential target stays. Since the mixed server is only measured in the full measurement elsewhere, slot 1 takes it in every pass of the search (`slotFidelity`). If the two cannot hold together, the tuner's report shows the miss and says which pair never meets. Slot 2 has **one** rule card, the boss's first, at the slot's low strength, and no Build-up. From slot 3 the boss has the full set (every card and its Build-up) at the slot's strength.

**A later lap's new card: the luck cards and Breakwater** (Misa, 2026-10-31, "Lucky Strike and Slippery both go on raid bosses" in `docs/design/decisions/raids.md`, and Breakwater beside Slippery on the Colossus the same day; the lead's placement, measured). A known boss's second lap is stronger by one new card, and the Tidal Colossus by two, a column of the track (`SLOT_EXTRA_CARDS` in `track.ts`, beside `SLOT_CARDS`; `slotBoss` passes it to `raidBossOf` as `extraCards`). An entry holds from its slot on every lap of that boss, at the slot's card strength:

| Card                                  | Boss            | Slots             | Answer                        |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Slippery** (flat dodge)             | Tidal Colossus  | 6, 11, 16, and on | Clear Skies (Halcyray, rare)  |
| **Breakwater** (wards cut its sweeps) | Tidal Colossus  | 6, 11, 16, and on | Barricade (Kilnmole, common)  |
| **Lucky Strike** (boss crits)         | Storm Sovereign | 8, 13, 18, and on | Cool Head (Thornbuck, common) |

The first lap is as before (slots 1 to 5 carry no extra card) and each card sits on one boss; only the Colossus has two. All three answers are ownable at every one of these slots (a mixed server of 8: Cool Head 100%, Clear Skies 100% from week 3, Barricade a common line), and the card's text reads "Its waves break on wards: Row Ward cuts against its sweeps count x2.4 to x3 (by strength)" (`wardBp` in `BossMods`, `cards.ts`: a ward's cut times 1 + 2 x the card's strength, so x2.44 at slot 6's 72% and x3 at slot 11's 100%, and at most 75% of a hit). Living Shelter, a beside-cut aura, is boosted the same way; only an area or Wind-up hit is cut by a ward at all. The boss's first written answer still copes once the slot is retuned (below). Nothing marks it as harder than any other card: a server finds it on the boss card (R24.8).

**Why these two bosses** (measured with `trackTuner.ts` and a team search at the slot's roster; `docs/design/decisions/raids.md` has the numbers). The theme fits (the Sovereign's bolts "strike", the Colossus is a body of slick water), and the numbers decide:

- **The first answer survives the card.** At the committed numbers, before any retune, the card lowers the slot's prepared potential (the Colossus at slot 6: 132% to 111% with Slippery; the Sovereign at slot 8: 155% to 128% with Lucky Strike) and its tool's worth; the tuner then asks whether numbers exist that meet every target again, and for these two bosses they do, at both laps, with no miss (slots 6, 8, 11 and 13: prepared 128% to 184%, tool 37 to 46 points, classic 72% to 84%). The other placements measured worse: on the Cinder Wyrm Slippery leaves slot 7's classic potential at the edge (70%) and slot 12's tool at 33 points against 35 with the Wyrm's numbers at their limits; Lucky Strike on the Wyrm cuts its tool to 22 points at slot 7 against 27; Slippery on the Sovereign leaves slot 8's tool at 29 against 30; and on Old Bogback either card cuts the prepared first-weekend kill from 98% to 42% to 60% at slots 9 and 14. So Old Bogback and the Cinder Wyrm carry no luck card.
- **Cool Head gets a job** (Thornbuck's other Knacks do little on the bosses): in a team search at slot 8 the best Cool Head line is worth about 9 points of potential, and 4 to 6 at slot 13. **Clear Skies is the weaker answer** (0 to 3 points at slot 6, 8 at slot 11): a reaction line (marks are never dodged: All In) beats Slippery on its own, which the card's text already allows.
- No card gets a written answer of its own (`BOSS_ANSWERS`): the first answers cope, and the card-level answers (`ANSWERS` in `report.ts`) are tested for ownability at every slot that carries the card (`test/ownability.test.ts`).

**Why Breakwater** (an overnight experiment on `combat-v2-breakwater-experiment`, option B; Misa chose it on 2026-10-31). With Slippery alone, the Colossus's second and third laps (slots 6 and 11) were out of reach for the mixed server at any HP where an unprepared party was not already winning every week: its casual columns fall to the sweeps, and the classic party was _stronger_ against the boss than the mixed server (the mixed kill was 0% and 1%, below). The card was built so that the prepared players' tools protect the casual ones:

- **Wards cover a whole row, whoever owns them.** Barricade (a Row Ward) and Living Shelter (a beside cut) cut the area and Wind-up hits that land on every creature in their row or beside them, so a prepared player's Kilnmole shelters the casual players in that row. Against Breakwater that cut is 2.4 to 3 times as large (a Row Ward's 15% becomes 37% to 45%).
- **The unprepared party has no ward and gains nothing.** The classic party carries none, so its potential and kill rate are unchanged by the card (80% to 84%, no kills at slots 6 and 11), and the tuner's classic limits still hold. The card only helps a mixed server in which someone has read the boss.
- **The alternative lost.** A column cut for a living Guard ("Tide Guard", option A) was measured too: the classic party already has a Guard in every column and gained more from it than the mixed server did, so it did not tell the prepared from the unprepared. It was dropped, with the experiment's harness.
- **The first answer still copes.** The dedicated server's line (Wide Reach Guards, the first written answer) meets every target at the retuned numbers (slot 6 15,600 HP / 113 Attack / 21 armour, slot 11 13,400 / 137 / 40, against 14,500 / 119 / 27 and 14,500 / 119 / 36 before: prepared 142% and 132% of the boss's HP). The mixed server fields the second answer, the Row Ward line (`SLOT_MIXED_ANSWER`, below), which the tuner picks on its own at both slots.

**Information measures** (not targets; the tuner's per-slot output and `--path` print them, `SlotInfo` in `trackTuner.ts`). All are damage potential at the expected speed, in the full measurement only:

- **Decent trios with rotated elements.** The classic party is one element mix, so it is element-lucky or element-unlucky against each boss. `ROTATED_TRIOS` are four decent unprepared parties, shown as the mean and range of their potential beside the classic party's. They were chosen from the common lines only, each with one Setter, one Payoff and one Guard, each form with its first own Knack, no boss reading, and with element mixes that differ from each other and from the classic party's: tide, ember and grove; spark, tide and ember; ember, tide and mire; spark, ember and mire.
- **The answer at middling ranks.** The prepared line with each player's creatures at their 8th to 11th best levels instead of their best (`MIDDLING_FROM`). A player with fewer creatures than the rank fields their weakest.
- **Prepared with random talents.** The prepared line with random legal talents, shown per slot (the `prepared potential (random)` column).
- **The builder server in the path** (above).

The evolution gate applies to all of them.

**Ownability** (`ownability.ts`): a slot's tool line must be ownable at the week the slot arrives (the chance that a mixed server of 8 owns every non-common line it uses is at least 50%) and now also **evolved**: every creature of the line is at or above its form's evolution level at that week, at the expected speed (a daily player's creature of its row). The report lists the creatures that are not, and the line fails. The lead decides what a failure changes: the line, the week or the rule.

**Determination** (after WoW's; `path.ts`, `DETERMINATION`; Misa, 2026-10-30, panel 6): each failed weekend on the same slot gives the server +5% damage and healing (`FightOptions.determinationBp`), **at most 5 stacks (+25%)**, reset by a kill; a Festival week changes nothing. It is **on by default from the second lap**: slot 6 onward. Slots 1 to 5 never get it (`Determination.fromSlot`). `--path` plays with it; `--path --determination` also prints the path without it as a comparison, and `--path --no-determination` prints only that. The track tuner never uses it (its path runs with `'off'`), so the table does not rely on it.

There is no numeric floor from the lap before: a later lap's cards are never weaker, its HP, Attack and armour may be lower when they make up for it, and the rule is that its first-weekend kill rate is not above the lap before's by more than noise (the tuner reports it). The lab tests' rules (`raidRules.ts`) stay with the three fixed ranks and are not applied to slots.

## Ownability

A raid answer must be built from creatures a server can own at the week the slot arrives (2026-10-30; Misa's constraint in the valley's round 15: it is fine to require a rare here or there, since players pool their rarer creatures in the raid roster, but an all-common requirement is too limiting, and epics should not be needed early).

- **Per line.** An evolved form cannot be found by scouts: a player owns it by owning its line's stage 1 and evolving it. So a form counts as its line, and the line has its stage 1 form's rarity. Rare lines: stormray (stormray, halcyray, tempestray), mossback (mossback, ancientshell, bloomback), coralynx. Epic lines: aurorix, gaiadrake, hearthshell, lanternfin, solmane.
- **The coverage.** `server-coverage.json` is **per creature line** (`unit: "line"`). For a mixed server of 5, 8 or 15 players and each week it gives the share of each rarity's lines that at least one player owns: the chance a given line of that rarity can be pooled into the raid roster. Each week has three sets of shares: `common`/`rare`/`epic` over all lines released so far (they dip at content drops, when lines nobody owns yet join the denominator), `forms` (the old per-form shares), and **`launch`**: the shares over the 14 lines of the launch content that scouts can find (8 common, 3 rare, 3 epic). The launch content has 16 lines; Lanternfin and Hearthshell are festival prizes (`catchable: false` in `content.creatures`), not in the scout pool and not in the coverage. Reading a launch share as the chance that a given line is owned is the approximation we accept.
- **Exact, not a lower bound.** The answer lines use only launch creatures, and a launch line, once owned, stays owned, so ownability reads the **`launch` share** as it is (`coverageAt`): exact per line and it never falls (a test keeps that). The earlier version read the all-forms share as its running maximum, a lower bound; that workaround is gone.
- **A festival prize is never ownable.** A line scouts cannot find (its stage 1 form is not `catchable`, read from `content.creatures`) has an ownable chance of 0. A boss must not need it; the report shows such a line as "festival prize". Today that is Hearthshell (Lanternfin is in no answer).
- **Every slot, for the line the tuner measures.** The rule holds on every slot, slot 1 included, for the line the track tuner measures as prepared: the boss's first answer in `BOSS_ANSWERS`, or the slot's own line when the track names one (`SLOT_LINE` in `track.ts`, read through `slotPreparedOf`, used by the tuner and the path). Slots 1 and 2 name one: a server there has stage 1 forms only (the evolution gate), and slot 1's boss's first answer also needs Tempestray, a rare that a server of 8 owns with a chance of 33% in week 1 (the valley's launch share of the 3 rare lines). Both lines are all common, so they pass on their own.
- **The rule.** A slot's **tool line** (the line the track tuner measures as "prepared") is **ownable** at the slot's arrival week when the chance that a mixed server of 8 owns every non-common line it uses is at least 50%. That chance is the product, over the distinct non-common lines in the answer, of that rarity's launch coverage at that week (0 for a festival prize). An all-common answer is always ownable. So one rare line is fine once rare coverage reaches 50%, two need about 71%, and an epic is out until late. The numbers are `OWNABLE_CHANCE_MIN` and `OWNABLE_SERVER` in `targets.ts`; the check is `ownability.ts`, shared by the test (`test/ownability.test.ts`) and the report (`--path` and the full report's track section print, per slot, the lines each answer needs, the chance on servers of 5, 8 and 15, and pass or fail).
- The slot's arrival week is the committed `SLOT_WEEKS` in `track.ts`: the weeks the table was tuned for, the dedicated server's median.
- **Slot 1's line**, at week 1 and the expected speed (60 weekends, written talents): the boss's first answer needs Tempestray, a rare, at the numbers tuned for it (16,700 HP / 107 Attack / 20 armour) it reached 129% potential and a first-weekend kill of 100%; the commons line (both Tempestrays replaced by Geyserpup with Self-Starter, Edge-walker kept on one) reached 90% at those numbers and 128% after a retune (Cindle 56%, Pyrelynx 66%, Geyserpup 75% without talents were worse).
