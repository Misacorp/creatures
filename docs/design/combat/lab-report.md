## Talents: best board with it against best board without it (gauntlet and raid row)

**The boss.** Its HP is 3,161, calibrated at report time so that the classic line kills a plain boss in about 8 of the 10 rounds on average, so the length of the fight matters (the boss of the other sections has a million HP, so fights always last ten rounds there).

**The score** of one fight, 0 to 100: 50 for the share of the boss's HP removed, 20 for a win, up to 15 for the rounds a win left unused (fewer rounds is better), and up to 15 for the share of the three creatures still standing (a knockout costs 5 points). A talent that saves creatures or ends the fight sooner shows it.

### The talents' share of the team's result

**What it is.** The share of a team's result that comes from talents: the best gauntlet team of each card (and the best raid board), each creature given the best-fitting talent from its line's pool (one talent each; in the raid one per form), against the same team with none, on a boss whose HP is tuned to that team, as a week's boss is tuned to the players: the team wins 40% of its fights without talents, a fight that could go either way (the boss of the per-talent table below is the classic line's 8-round boss, which these teams crush: they score about 88 of 100 there and no talent could show 20%); the 0 to 100 score; share = 1 - score without / score with. The target is about 20% on average (accepted 17% to 23%); a casual roster, with a random talent from each creature's whole pool (idle ones included), should gain something but stay well below the best fit (at most 60% of it), so talents stay situational rather than flat (Misa, 2026-10-28). The raid board is scored by the best-teams section's damage score on a boss with a million HP (the raid has no calibrated boss), talents chosen on other seeds than the ones scored.

**Team share check: pass.**

**The boards.** Every form whose line's pool has the talent and whose role it fits, with no Knack on the measured form (the Mire talents carry the line's Mire Knack, with and without, because they do nothing without one). The form stands in each of the three slots with two fixed fitting partners in either order, for two partner sets (the natural team's partners, and the classic trio's two other roles with their classic Knacks): up to 12 legal boards. The score is the best board with the talent and the best board without it, each chosen on its own, on every card (24 seeds each, the same seeds for every board), averaged over those forms and cards. Gain is the difference in points; the percentage is against the best score without it and reads n/a when that is under 10 points. Knockouts are per fight (with / without), on the best board of each. Nothing here forces a board for a talent: a position talent pays for standing where it wants to.

**Raid gain.** The raid-shaped talents (Shoulder to Shoulder, Odd One Out, Edge-walker, Front-liner, Slow Burn, Finisher, Rally Cry, Flank Guard, Long Shadow, Kindred Boost) also run on a raid row: 3 rows of 12, the classic trio in order with one slot in four a creature of the form's own element (so neighbour talents have kin to read), and one creature of the form in one slot. The boss has Great Wave (every second round) and Payoff Hunter, and 51,159 HP (the classic row removes 90% of it in ten rounds, so a win never hides a small gain and a Finisher has its last rounds). The form is tried in 15 slots (ends, near the ends and the middle, in every row) and one random slot, 24 seeds each, with the talent and without it on the same seeds, so a slot is compared with itself. The score of a fight is the damage of the creature and the creatures within 2 slots of it in its row, weighted by how many of them still stand: one creature of 36 is too small a share of the raid to read through the noise of a fight. The raid gain is the gain at the best slot for the talent (where it gains most), in percent of the score without it, scaled by 1.67 (the five of the neighbourhood over the three of the gauntlet) to read on the gauntlet's scale; the random slot's gain is shown beside it. A raid-shaped talent is judged on the raid gain; the others on the gauntlet gain.

**Holds** is the share of fights in which the talent's condition held or its trigger fired at least once, on the form's natural board (every card, no board chosen for it) and in a row 12 wide (the form at a random slot among mixed classic creatures, plain card, a boss with four times the HP; this is the raid rate for position talents). Flat stats always hold. **Best card** is where it gains most, as a share of the score without it. A talent that is top of its role on most cards is flagged "best on most cards" and is a new default pick.

### Boss Rank: the same weekends at a low, a mid and the high rank

Every rule card and boss gate has a strength from 0% to 100% (`strengthBp`), and the strength rises with the server's Boss Rank (Misa, 2026-10-29). Reflection returns that share of the reaction's bonus; Shake It Off clears that share of the Mired; Reaction and Element Resistance cut that share of what they cut at full strength; an Escalating step grows the boss by that share; a silence hits that share of its slots and lasts that share of its turns; Judgement Bolt hits for that share of the Payoff's Max HP (a one-shot at full strength, a heavy hit below it); half of a boss's armour is its hide and the other half is its card, the strength's share. A card *asks* for a tool at low strength and *requires* it at high strength. The three ranks are **low** (about rank 2: cards at 30%), **mid** (about rank 5: cards at 65%), **high** (about rank 10: cards at 100%). HP and Attack follow each boss's ladder (`ranks` in its spec; the high rank is the spec), and every step down is softer in every respect: a lower rank has less HP, less Attack, softer cards and, for Thornmother, a thinner bark. The server's roster grows with the rank, as in the shipped game (the shipped table's early ranks are small because the roster is small): the lab's creatures are level 12 at the low rank, 18 at the mid rank, 25 at the high rank, the casual players' levels scaled the same way (a creature's stats grow with its level: a level 12 creature has about 70% of a level 25 one's). Without this the lab's level 25 roster would make every low rank boss a walkover unless it had more HP than the high rank's. 60 weekends each, 40 draws of the mixed and casual servers, 10 draws of random talents. Cells read the share of the boss's HP removed (sd between weekends), then tier 1 / tier 2 / kill.

## Not ported from the shipped bosses and foes

- Boss Rank: the rank-gated moves (rank 4 and 7) and the HP growth per rank. Every move is in play; Boss Rank scaling is the simulator phase (roadmap 5).
- Turn orders (left to right, serpentine, right to left): the lab acts row by row, left to right, with a boss turn after each row (R8).
- Phase 1, Phase 2 and the twelve-round finale, with their retreat lines: a weekend is three stages of eight rounds that share the boss’s HP (R14). The retreat lines are live-finale flavour.
- The lance’s falloff (each row softer) and the Guard that stops a lance: a column hits every creature in it at the move’s power, and a Guard covers by reach like any area hit.
- Mired on creatures (the mud bosses’ Wild Sweep, Barrage, Front Slam and Quake): the lab has Mired only on the boss (-1.5% damage and dodge per stack). A creature debuff needs a debuff-resistance tool first, so it is a proposal question.
- Purge as a move: it is the Shake It Off card, every third round instead of every fifth.
- Finale abilities (Rally, Shield Wall, Purge): gone with R18’s Knacks.
- Slag Tyrant’s backlash (half the time a reaction burns its Payoff): it is Reflective Hide, which is certain (P-04), plus an escalation that thickens its shell.
- Room modifiers (Flooded, Thorns and the rest), rare rooms, carved doors, loot, experience and the Sealed Vault: a run here is the shipped map (fights, elites, mystery rooms, caches, the camp and the floor boss) and the Undercroft below it. Keepsakes are applied as shares off the foe, not as team stats.


## The boss track: ownability

### Ownability of the tool lines

A slot's tool line (the line the tuner measures: the boss's first answer, or the slot's own line) is ownable at the slot's arrival week when the chance that a mixed server of 8 owns every non-common line it uses is at least 50%. The chance is the product, over the distinct non-common lines, of that rarity's launch coverage at that week (`server-coverage.json`, `launch`; an evolved form counts as its line, with the rarity of the line's first stage). A festival prize (a line scouts cannot find) has a chance of 0. The tool line must also be evolved: every creature of it at or above its form's evolution level at that week (a daily player's creature of its row, expected speed), or it is fielded as stage 1 forms. Arrival weeks: the committed `SLOT_WEEKS` (1 2 3 4 6 7 8 9 11 12 13 14 16 17 18), the weeks the track was tuned for (the week of the first fight, never a Festival week).

## What this cannot tell

- It is a prototype: gear, heal crits on screen and the raid board UI are simplified or missing; talents are measured one at a time in the per-talent table, and as a share of the team result (one talent per creature, never searched with the Knacks).
- Mire has no Setter or Payoff yet, so its reactions never happen here.
- Searches are random plus local improvement; a missing team is not proof that it is weak.
- The raid search uses 3 rows of 4 (four players); real servers place up to 15 per row, first come first served. The real bosses have their own arenas (2 to 4 rows of 4).
- The real bosses and foes are lab data tuned to this report: the best teams and the intended answers are found by seeded searches over a weekend or a run, so a missing team is not proof that it is weak, and the answers are lines written down from those searches. Talent answers are read against the classic party with the same talent.
- Fun is not in the numbers. Use this to find outliers and dead options, then test feel in the game.
