# The commons: idling together for the whole server

Status: **proposed**, revised after the panel (`reviews/2026-10-30-commons.md`). Decided by Misa after the panel: every place has its own open project, with a guided start (round 12, Q30). Accepted by Misa so far: one shared valley where personal and shared progress take turns as the limit (round 1, Q1); gifts counting in full (round 2, Q4); a max level per place, then cosmetic projects, each level harder than the last (round 4, Q12); the bonfire as a communal hearth (Q11); the item level cap raised by many systems, including "really rare finds that players have to collect five of different types, like a Stardew Valley type of thing where you upgrade the community house" (round 11, Q27). Evidence: `docs/research/idle-job-posts.md` ("Co-op idle in a small server"), `docs/research/idle-coop-raids.md`, `docs/research/gear-lottery.md`.

## The question

Misa: "Could players offer some of their own resources for the common good? Could they have creatures out performing tasks that benefit the entire server, not just themselves? Can the places where jobs are done on a server themselves be improved in a communal way, through a ton of common resources or the extremely rare find for specifically this purpose?"

## The idea: three ways to give, one valley to build

The valley's places (the Forge, the Signpost, the Paddock, the School, the bonfire) belong to the whole server. Each has a **server level**, raised by **projects** built from many common gifts. Each also has **collections**: sets of rare finds that the server gathers and sets into the building, Stardew Valley style. And everything the server builds warms the **Forge's fire**: the item level cap of everything made or found (`gear.md` V-GEAR-10). Players give in three ways, all optional:

1. **Resources** on a place's project board (a crate, a scaffold, a donation bowl): world materials (scrap and shards) plus Acorns (round 14, Q33). Essence goes to creatures, not buildings.
2. **Creatures** on communal seats, whose work goes to the server's project (haulers at the Forge, road menders at the Signpost, a lamplighter at the bonfire). The creature earns its own XP and skill there.
3. **Rare finds** that fill a collection: a find made for the purpose, turned up by scouts, the dungeon, the raid or the fair. A find sets itself into its socket when it is found.

## Rules

### Projects and place levels

- **V-COM-1** Everyone benefits from a place's level, whether or not they gave (F-6). A level adds seats per player (V-COM-10), recipe tiers or better odds, and a visible change to the building (a second chimney, a longer road, banners).
- **V-COM-2** Giving is recognised, never ranked: each level keeps a plaque of everyone who helped build it; no amounts and no order by amount. Each giver privately sees their own part ("you laid 31% of this chimney"). In the return walk, your own part comes first (V-RET-5).
- **V-COM-3** The thank-you is a keepsake: anyone who gave anything, or had a creature on a communal seat while the project was built, gets the same small keepsake for their house and a name on the plaque. It has no value to farm, so there is no threshold and no rule against new accounts.
- **V-COM-4** Projects never fail, decay or expire (F-1); a finished level is kept for good.
- **V-COM-5** Size and pace. Gifts always count in full (round 2, Q4). A project's size is fixed when it opens and scales with the server's active players, counted by how often they visited in the last 7 days (a weekly player counts about 0.4), more slowly than the player count, so a lone player can finish one in weeks. A finished project builds for about three visible days before the next one opens, so a hoard raises one level, never several in a day. Every place has its own open project, and the server chooses together where to put its gifts (round 12, Q30: "let's not take the agency out of players' hands"). Each place builds for three days between its levels, so a hoard raises at most one level per place.
- **V-COM-6** The scene shows progress: scaffolding rises as gifts come in, and creatures on communal seats are seen working. Names are not shown in the scene; what a project still needs shows on a tap, worded as an invitation.
- **V-COM-7** A newcomer joins a valley already built up and benefits from day one; the open project is open to them at once (F-7).
- **V-COM-8** Cheap to run (F-9): one row per project, one per player per project, one per collection socket; gifts are single writes; a daily job writes the active players' weights and the fire's time floor into the fire's row; duplicates of finds are counts.
- **V-COM-10** Personal and shared progress take turns being the limit (round 1, Q1): a place's level sets each player's limit (anvils, scout seats, School seats); each player unlocks their own up to it, each seat's price rising with the place's level.
- **V-COM-11** Each place has a max level (example: 5); each level's project is bigger than the last. After the max, projects are cosmetic (a weathervane, statues, seasonal decorations everyone sees, G-7) and still warm the fire a little. New places can open as content at any time.

- **V-COM-15** A guided start for a new server (round 12, Q30). The valley opens with one place's project (example: the bonfire's hearth), small enough to finish in a few days, so the whole server pools into it and learns how building works. When it is done, a second place's project opens; when those two have levelled, every place's project is open and the server chooses together. Every first level is small (finished within the first two or three weeks); the size grows from level 2 on.

### Collections

- **V-COM-12** Each place has one collection at launch (more come as content): a board in the building with five sockets, each for a particular rare find of a different kind (example: the Signpost's "Stones of the Roads"). Each socket shows its find's source and its odds or clock, so a server can go after it on purpose, and at least one socket in every collection is filled by ordinary idle play (a scout's everyday finds, a resting creature's small moment), so every player can fill one.
  - Rare finds come from scouts' deeper areas (V-SCOUT-6), each place's keystone clock (V-SCOUT-9; the clock runs whether or not a scout is posted, and a ready keystone waits for the next scout posted there), dungeon landmarks, the raid's finale and the fair's Prize Stall. A find is useful only in its socket, never a currency.
  - A find for an open socket sets itself when it is found, with its finder's name for good, and is the return walk's surprise ("This belongs at the Signpost!"). A copy of a find already set melts into a little Essence.
  - A completed collection gives the place something its projects cannot (a new recipe family at the Forge, a far road at the Signpost, a visiting teacher's chair at the School), warms the Forge's fire, and is told in the return walk as a server moment. Collections never expire.

### The Forge's fire: the item level cap

- **V-COM-13** The item level cap (`gear.md` V-GEAR-8, V-GEAR-10) is the Forge's fire, drawn as a flame that grows, with a picture of what comes next ("better scarves soon") and its numbers behind a tap. In one sentence: everything the server builds or beats makes the fire hotter, and a hotter fire makes better gear.
  - **What the Forge makes** comes out at the fire's level, so every warming lifts the next pieces (V-FORGE-7). Found gear comes at its source's level (the dungeon's depth, the finale's rank), never above the fire.
  - **What warms it**, each by a step of one or more levels: a place's level-up (the Forge's the most), a completed collection, the first kill of each boss on combat v2's track, the server's creatures earning Master titles (every few titles), and cosmetic projects (a little).
  - **A floor that rises with time**: the fire is never below a level that rises each week, so a server that can't beat a raid boss still moves; time adds nothing for a server already above it.
  - It never falls. The tap shows the next modifier threshold and the last five things that warmed it.

### Communal seats

- **V-COM-14** Communal seats (`posts.md` V-POST-13): one per player per place (example). Their work fills the open project's bar as a steady trickle, worked out lazily and credited when the contributor visits (V-POST-14). The communal seat is an extra seat, never one of the player's own (round 12, Q31; F-11). Misa is unsure it will be fun ("Probably not. Who knows? If you feel like building it, then let's just roll with it"), so it is tested in the phase 3 prototype before it is built.
- **V-COM-9** Moved: the optional weekly festival goal is a later idea (`later.md`).

## Example numbers (the economy lab sets the real ones)

A server of 8 daily players, each earning about 50 Acorns a day and giving about a third of it, plus communal seats: about 150 Acorns' worth a day into projects.

| What                          | Example                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A project                     | the first about two weeks of the server's giving (about 2,000 Acorns' worth); each next x1.25; three build days between                           |
| A new server's first projects | the first place's level 2 in about 3 days of giving; the second in about a week; the rest open by week 2 or 3                                     |
| Place levels by week 12       | about five or six level-ups across the valley, wherever the server put them (all five at level 2, or one place at level 4)                        |
| A collection                  | five finds; the first done in about three to four weeks                                                                                           |
| The fire's start              | 10 on a new server                                                                                                                                |
| The fire's floor              | +3 a week (46 at week 12)                                                                                                                         |
| Warmth                        | a place level-up +3 (the Forge's +5); a collection +4; a boss's first kill +2; every 5 Master titles +1; a cosmetic project +1                    |
| Modifier thresholds           | 2 pips from 10, 3 from 25, 4 from 50, 5 from 80 (`gear.md`)                                                                                       |
| The fire at week 12           | about 50 for a server that kills its bosses, about 46 (the floor) for one that kills none: 4-pip lines in reach for both, 5-pip lines months away |
