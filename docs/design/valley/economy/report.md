# The economy lab report

> Generated by `pnpm --filter @creatures/econ report` from `packages/econ`. Never edit by hand: change `src/rules.ts` and run it again.
> 20 seeds per scenario, 24 weeks, hour by hour. Numbers are medians over seeds and players; a bracket (a-b) is the 10th to 90th percentile.
> Combat is not simulated: the dungeon and raid pay fixed inputs (see the appendix). Every rate is an example from the system docs unless the appendix says otherwise.

## 1. The targets

- **PASS** T-1: of the creatures a daily player has owned for two weeks or more, 100% are within 5 levels of their 3rd best at week 4 (worst 4 levels below the 3rd best, median 2, worst without those held at the evolution level 2) and 73% at week 8 (worst 10 levels below the 3rd best, median 3, worst without those held at the evolution level 6); the 6th creature (a second team's best) is 2 below the 3rd at week 8. An optimiser's share at week 4 is 100% (pass). It also depends on how fast creatures arrive: at the docs' 2% an hour (the default here is 1.3%) a set-and-forget player's share at week 4 is 100% (pass) and an optimiser's 100% (pass). Target: creatures owned two weeks or more within 5 levels of the 3rd best at weeks 4 and 8, and two strong teams at week 8.
- **PASS** T-2a: an optimiser visiting three times a day gets 103% of an optimiser visiting once in XP and 100% in posts' and the allowance's output over 24 weeks. In the model extra visits add no rate (idle gains run at the same rate however often a player looks in), so this passes by construction: it shows only that nothing in the rules pays a frequent visitor more, not that choices are worth little. Target: under 110%.
- **PASS** T-2b: a weekend-off player gets 98% of a daily player's idle growth (effective hours, weeks 1 to 12). Target: about 100% (a weekend off costs almost nothing).
- **PASS** T-2c: a weekly player's XP per creature, catch-up included, is 82% of a daily player's (idle gains alone: 79%; with fights: 83%; their best creature is level 27 at week 12 against a daily player's 29). Their scouts bring home 41% of a daily player's finds per seat (stores of three days: about 72 of 168 hours). Target: about 75% to 80% of a daily player's XP per creature (goals.md T-2).
- **PASS** T-2d: a player back after four weeks away keeps every level (best creature +4 levels vs before) and their 3rd-best creature is within 2 levels of the server's middle after 10 days of visits (the target is about 14; if catch-up also boosted fight XP: 5 days). Target: recognises the roster; within about two weeks of daily visits of the server's middle (V-BON-2).
- **PASS** T-3: a server of 8 lifts its first place a level on day 7.3; its first collection is done in week 4 (the docs expect weeks 3 to 4) and 4 collections are done by week 12 (no project needs one). Target: first level within 14 days; the rare-find route matters (at least one collection by week 12) without being required.
- **PASS** T-3 (pace): by week 12 a server of 8 has 5 place level-ups across the valley and a fire of 47 if it kills every boss (64 at week 23). Target: about 5 to 6 level-ups by week 12; the fire about 50 at week 12 and under about 100 before week 24 (commons.md).
- **PASS** T-4: in the worst week (weeks 2 to 12) a typical player's anvils tinker for want of scrap 0% of the time (daily, week 2). Target: at most 10% for want of scrap.
- **INFO** T-4 (shards): for want of a shard of the element the team needs, the worst week is 88% (weekly, week 4); averaged over weeks 2 to 12 that profile loses 72%. With the Forge floor full the worst week is 0% (daily, week 2), mean 0%. Target: goals.md T-4 names scrap only; shards are shown for the same 10%.
- **PASS** T-8: a daily player owns 11 lines at week 4 and 25 at week 12 (with the forms they evolved into: 15 and 44); 100% of the starting pool's commons are owned by week 6; at week 24 41% of the rares and 20% of the epics released are owned. Target: about 9 to 12 lines at week 4, 20 to 28 at week 12, most commons within 6 weeks, rares over months (15% to 60% by week 24), epics rarely (at most 35%).
- **PASS** T-9: 2 creatures of a daily player wait at their evolution level for Essence at week 12. Target: at most 3: Essence must not be starved to keep the pile down.
- **PASS** T-7: 0 of a daily player's 4 resources (Acorns, scrap, shards, Essence) are dead (a pile rising 4 weeks running to 4 weeks of what the resource is spent on) before week 12. Target: none (resources.md: nothing that only piles up).
- **PASS** T-6: a daily player's best creature is level 27 at week 10 and 29 at week 12; it reaches level 30 on day 89. Target: still growing in week 12 (example: level 29 of 30), not capped by week 10.

(T-5, that the best team differs across bosses, is checked in combat v2's lab.)

## 2. The resource ledger

Per spendable resource, in the mixed server of 8 (a daily and a weekly player shown; every profile is in `report-data.json`). "In" and "out" are per week; stock is at the end of weeks 1, 2, 4, 8, 12 and 24; waste is what a full store did not pick up over 24 weeks; "days to next" is how long until the player can afford the next thing worth buying (median over weeks 2 to 12; "-" means there was nothing to buy that week); choices is the things a player could do at a visit, counted under each resource it needs. ### The weekly visitor (V-VIS)

He is in the valley from Thursday 00:00 to the raid's finale on Sunday evening; a player meets him only on a visit in that window. He rerolls every modifier line of a worn piece for Acorns and scrap: 60 Acorns and 400 scrap at item level 10, linear in the piece's item level, times 1.5 for each reroll that player already made that week (reset weekly), and one rotating rare blueprint for 40 Acorns and 50 shards of any element (from the most-stocked elements first), so the shard pile has a sink too. A set-and-forget player rerolls once a week, on the best epic it wears, keeping 100 Acorns and 300 scrap; an optimiser rerolls epics, then rares, until the next price would pass its reserves or its pieces are good (an epic with 4 of its 5 lines at the top pip, a rare with 2 of 3). The lab does not model what a line does: a piece has 1 / 3 / 5 lines by rarity, each a pip tier from what its item level allows (the top tier with chance 0.3).

## Appendix: assumptions and simplifications

### Assumptions (from `src/rules.ts`, each marked `// ASSUMPTION:` there)

- `beyond`: "near zero after about a month" is 2%.
- `catchUp`: catch-up multiplies idle XP only, not what fights pay (V-BON-2 says "XP only, never output"; a sensitivity run applies it to fights too).
- `fitShare`: how often a seat's creature happens to fit its post. A set-and-forget player places a creature once and looks at the icon about a third of the time; an optimiser matches almost always.
- `masterSmithScrapPerk`: a Master Smith brings 5% more scrap from the player's scouts ("a little more", V-FORGE-7).
- `undercroftShare`: combat is not simulated, so this share of dungeon runs counts as an Undercroft clear (or 2+ levels deep).
- `scoutPrices`: the first seat at each place is free; the rest cost these Acorns (the docs: "about three weeks of Acorn income in all", about 1,000 for the seats a player reaches by week 8).
- `anvilScrap`: an anvil also takes scrap ("own anvils unlocked with their own Acorns and scrap", V-FORGE-6).
- `price`: about a week of Acorn income each (posts.md), any kind.
- `tongs`: tongs make common pieces a third faster ("faster common pieces", no number in the docs).
- `loupe`: the loupe is slower and lifts the rare and epic odds.
- `creatures`: 0.75% an hour, not the docs' 2%: with 2% a daily player has all 34 forms by week 8, and posts.md's worked month ("15 creatures by week 4") needs about 0.75%. The two numbers in the docs disagree; this one follows the worked month.
- `rareFinds`: the "everyday" rare find that fills one socket of a collection (V-COM-12) about every 28 scout-days.
- `destinations`: six destinations, profiles picked to cover scrap, shards, creatures and rare finds (places.md lists them).
- `placeAffinity`: a destination whose element a creature has doubles its weight when a new creature arrives (the shipped 2x).
- `species`: a content drop adds 12 lines, 6 common, 4 rare and 2 epic (`drop`; the earlier 6/4/2 kept, now counted as lines), with elements cycling through the five; and a line's typical number of forms by rarity is `forms` (a common line 3, as most today; a rare line 2; an epic line 1, as today's epics), so a drop is about 28 forms.
- `rarity`: arrivals weigh a line by its rarity, so commons come at the "week 4 pace" and rarer lines take months.
- `lureOwnedWeight`: a lure halves the weight of species the player already owns.
- `blueprintPool`: known blueprints of a pool this size; a known one found again is scrap (shipped: 5).
- `duplicateCreatureScrap`: a duplicate creature takes the Recommended answer, which pays this much scrap (the docs name no amount).
- `exchange`: an "exchange crit" gives `critYield` instead of 1 with this chance (Misa's example, 10% for 2).
- `baseOdds`: odds of a plain piece's rarity (the shipped game's crafting is 'common' unless reached).
- `floorPiecesPerAnvil`: the floor holds about three days of pieces (V-FORGE-5): ceil(72 / 16) per anvil.
- `slotsPerCreature`: three gear slots per creature (the shipped game's Hat, Scarf and Charm).
- `weakMargin`: a slot is "weaker" (so "for my team" makes pieces for it) when its item level is this far below the fire.
- `dismantle`: what dismantling a piece gives, from the shipped ranges' middles (scrap is about 40% of the craft's 6).
- `tidyAt`: a set-and-forget player bulk-dismantles when the bag reaches this many; an optimiser every visit.
- `foundGearOdds`: scouts' finds and the dungeon's drops carry rarity with the same odds as a plain craft.
- `giveReserveScrap`: a player keeps this much scrap and this many of each shard for the Forge (and the exchange) before giving the rest.
- `reachReserveEssence`: an optimiser keeps this much Essence for evolutions before it reaches for rarity.
- `allowancePerDay`: posts.md says "about 50 a day" for a daily player (the review said 150); split as below so a daily player gets 30 + 10 + 2 + 10 = 52. The allowance builds up idly (away steps apply) in a store of the same size as a post's.
- `start`: a new player starts with this many Acorns (the shipped welcome kit gives scrap and a blueprint, no Bait amount is in the docs).
- `team`: the three best creatures fight, as in posts.md's worked month ("her best three also run the dungeon and the raid").
- `maxPasses`: one run a day; passes stack to three (posts.md "dungeon passes").
- `acorns`: what a dungeon run pays besides XP, from the shipped room rewards (a run is about ten rooms: scrap 1 to 4 each, a boss's Essence, a landmark's glimmer now and then, a full clear's gear piece about half the time).
- `xpPerWeek`: about 70 XP a day averaged is 490 a week per fighting creature, for visiting between Friday and Sunday.
- `essenceKill`: Essence from a week's raid: a kill pays more than the phases alone (shipped flat 10 + 15 + 25, +30% pool). TUNED 2026-10-30 (round 15): were 70 and 25 (35 and 12.5 in this round's first pass).
- `levelFactor`: level 1 to 2 costs 35% of the base (V-COM-15: "small enough to finish in a few days"), then the base, x1.25, x1.25.
- `cosmeticFactor`: cosmetic projects after the max level cost twice the base, repeating.
- `sizeExponent`: size grows with the active weight W as (W / 8) ^ 0.7 ("more slowly than the player count").
- `weight`: a player's weight by visit days in the last 7: 0.4 for one day (a weekly player), 1 from four days.
- `giftValue`: what one Acorn, one scrap and one shard are worth toward a project, in Acorns' worth (the project's size unit). Scrap is the plentiful one (a daily player earns about three times more of it than Acorns), so it counts half.
- `costSplit`: a project's cost is split by value into an Acorn part, a scrap part and a shard part in these shares.
- `placeElement`: the shard part of a place's project takes shards of this element (a simple tie of element to place); shards of any other element count at the exchange's rate (3 for 1), so one scarce element never holds a project back.
- `placeWeights`: the server's gifts pick an open place by these weights (the guided start overrides at first).
- `communalPerHour`: a communal seat's output toward its place's project, per effective hour (commons.md: "a steady trickle").
- `boards`: which five finds each place's board wants (commons.md examples: the Forge's Heartcoal from each element's place, the Signpost's stones from deeper areas, the bonfire's embers from the dungeon's landmarks, the Paddock's tokens from rare residents, the School's pages of one set), each with one ordinary-play socket as V-COM-12 requires.
- `landmarkPerRun`: a dungeon run turns up one of the four landmark embers with this chance (about one a week for 8 daily players).
- `pageInSetChance`: only this share of the journal pages scouts find belong to the School's set (there are several sets), uniform over its pages.
- `residentPerArrival`: a creature arriving is a rare resident with its token with this chance.
- `meltEssence`: a find that no open socket wants melts into this much Essence.
- `topPipChance`: a line is the top pip its piece's item level allows with this chance, else a lower tier with equal chances. Chosen so about 3 rerolls on a player's epics give a 4-of-5 top-pip epic about 8% of the time (the visitor panel's estimate).
- `baseLevel`: the price of a reroll is linear in the piece's item level: this much Acorns and scrap at item level `baseLevel` (the doc's example: "a base of 60 Acorns and 400 scrap"), times 1.5 for each reroll already made that week by that player.
- `reserveAcorns`: a player does not spend below these piles at his stall (Acorns for seats, kit and evolutions; scrap for pieces).
- `maxRerolls`: the most rerolls an optimiser makes in one week.
- `goodTop`: an optimiser rerolls an epic until 4 of its 5 lines are the top pip, a rare until 2 of its 3 (a set-and-forget player rerolls once a week on the best epic that is not there yet).
- `blueprintAcorns`: the week's rotating rare blueprint costs this many Acorns and this many shards of ANY element (taken from the most-stocked elements first); a player buys it when they can afford it above the Acorn reserve and know fewer than the pool.

### Simplifications

- Combat is not simulated. The dungeon pays 120 XP, 10 Acorns, 10 scrap and a little Essence per run (one pass a day, up to three stacked), the raid 490 XP per fighting creature for a week with a visit between Friday and Sunday, and its Essence depends on the server's boss dial (all, half or none). The three best creatures fight.
- A creature's post does not change how fast it grows (V-POST-3), so creatures are not tracked per post: the lab counts scout seats, anvils and communal seats as long as the player has creatures to stand on them (the first scout and anvil need two of the three starters).
- Fit is an average (a set-and-forget player's creatures fit about 30% of the time, an optimiser's 90%), not a per-creature draw; kit tilts are expected values; scrap, shards, Acorns and XP are expected values and only creatures, gear, blueprints, pages, keystones and rare finds are seeded draws.
- Modifier lines are minimal: a worn piece has 1, 3 or 5 lines by rarity, each only a pip tier (the top tier its item level allows with a fixed chance, else a lower one), rolled when the piece is worn and again at each reroll; the visitor's reroll redraws all of them and never keeps the old ones. Gear slots are open from level 1 (there are no slot unlock levels in the lab). A piece in the bag has no lines, and a worn piece is never moved to another creature.
- Mastery is counted per seat (the effective hours a seat has worked), not per creature, and a Master never moves on; the Master Scout perk (a blueprint at the Forge) is not modelled.
- The School and Paddock have levels, projects and warmth but no seats or output (the docs leave them open until combat v2 and `school.md`); push on, deep areas, opening hours of the Reef and the week's twist are not modelled.
- Gear is counts and item level only: three slots per creature, pieces at the fire's level, a slot is "weaker" when its item level is more than 6 below the fire. Gear stats, what a modifier line does, blueprint quality and the loupe's effect beyond a higher rare and epic share are not modelled; blueprints are counted, not required by orders.
- The scout pool is LINES: scouts find a line's stage 1, never an evolved form (evolving at level 15 gives the next form, for Essence: 20 for a common line and 40 for a rare one in the content, scaled by the tuned base). The content has 16 lines (34 forms); 14 can be found by scouts (8 common, 3 rare, 3 epic) and the two festival epics (Lanternfin, Hearthshell) cannot. A content drop adds 12 lines (6 common, 4 rare, 2 epic) every 4 weeks from week 4, a line having 3, 2 or 1 forms by rarity (assumed). Arrivals and duplicates use the lines available that week and weigh a line by rarity; places lean toward their element. A player keeps one creature of a line (duplicates become scrap) and so owns at most one evolved form of it: a line's two evolution branches are not both collected.
- The lab keeps 5 elements and 6 destinations. More elements will mean more kinds of shard (every Forge piece takes a shard of its element, so each new element is one more chain a player must cover with scouts) and more destinations to cover them; the shard stalls shown here would grow with them.
- Catch-up applies to idle XP only (not to fights). Its multiplier is fixed at each visit and holds until the next one (Q35 B), but never above the target it was fixed for: it fades as the creature closes the gap, so a player who visits less often cannot earn more of it than one who visits daily. A player who is away is not counted in the server's middle after seven days.
- Project gifts are chosen by weight among the open projects; a gift never exceeds what the project still needs, the rest stays with the giver. A project's cost is an Acorn part, a scrap part and a shard part (Q33), each filled by gifts from what a player has above a reserve for their own Forge; shards go to a project only as shards of the element tied to its place; Essence never goes to projects and journal pages are never spent. A find fills only its own collection socket; a spare melts into Essence. Sources of the sockets that are not keystones (dungeon landmarks, resident tokens, pages) use made-up rates.
- Ribbons are dropped (Misa): levels never stop. Honours (trials.md) replace them and gate nothing. Creatures are not tracked per post, so the lab places creatures on seats itself: a new seat takes a creature not at a post yet (fewest hours of work, then highest level), a set-and-forget player never moves anyone, and an optimiser moves a Master on and puts an unworked creature in its seat. A creature's hours of work, the dungeon and raid clears it was in and the finds it brought home tick its trial boxes. Combat is not simulated: a share of dungeon runs (an assumption) count as an Undercroft clear, and the raid party is the same team that fights; an optimiser gives the team's last place to the highest-level creature that still needs a fight box, a set-and-forget player does not.
- The cost estimate counts rows from the shape of a visit (one player row, the creature rows, seat rows, the valley's rows); it is not a measured query plan.
- T-5 (the best team differs across bosses) belongs to combat v2's lab.

### Policies

- **Set-and-forget** places a catch once, keeps its first kit, rotates "for my team" orders through the elements the shelf can supply, tidies the bag only when it nears full, and never reaches. **Optimiser** re-tilts every seat each visit to what it needs (scrap, a missing shard, a new species), keeps the kit that suits, reaches for rarity with spare Essence and bulk-dismantles every visit.
- **The shard exchange (Q34 B)** trades 3 shards of any other element for 1 of the one needed (a 10% chance of 2). An optimiser exchanges at every visit for what its orders need; a set-and-forget player only when an anvil is waiting on a shard at that visit (the one-tap fix).
- Both buy seats and anvils as soon as the place's level and the Acorns allow, then kit with Acorns to spare (a set-and-forget player buys two items, an optimiser all five), give their share of income and spend Essence on evolutions first.
