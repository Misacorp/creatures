# The truth: what lies under the valley

Status: **draft, not accepted**. The facts are the lead's to keep; nothing here ships as written.

**Spoilers.** This is the whole iceberg. Players see none of it directly, ever: they see objects, habits and half-heard remarks that only make sense if this is true. Read `README.md` in this folder before using it. Never quote or paraphrase this file in player text, PR descriptions, commit messages, patch notes, Discord posts or pages for Misa (unless he asked for spoilers).

To read one fact without the rest: `node .claude/skills/lore/check.mjs --fact S05` prints that fact and its ledger rows. The one-breath summary is at the very bottom of this file, on purpose.

Each fact has an ID. A fact is **sealed** until the chapter named in `arc.md` opens it, and "open" still means shown, never told (see the skill). Dev names in _italics_ are for us only: the game never uses them.

## The facts

### S01. Somebody lived here before

The valley was full once. The roads, the well, the forge, the hut on the Games meadow, the cellars under the Cave and the five far places where the raids are fought were all built by earlier explorers: ordinary folk with creatures, like the players. Not a lost race, not a greater age. Dev name: _the first valley_. The game never names them; it only shows their work, which is always practical, mended, and made for more hands than are here now (benches too long, hooks too many, a kettle for forty).

### S02. The low night

Past the far edge of the valley lies the Verge, where the night sky carries on underneath. One autumn the dark began to come up through the ground at that edge. It did not hurt anything. It made things drift: a path would forget where it went, a creature would wander out at dusk and not think to turn round. Dev name: _the low night_. Forgetting is what Void does, the way wet is what Tide does. "Nobody remembers the way" is not a figure of speech here.

### S03. The walkers and the promise

When enough creatures had drifted off, a party of explorers and their creatures walked out at the Verge after them. Dev name: _the walkers_. Those who stayed (_the stayers_) made one promise: to keep a light on, so the walkers could find the valley from the dark. Everything old and warm in the valley is a piece of that promise.

### S04. The five waylights

The stayers set five great lamps on the five high and far places around the valley, so it could be seen from every side: the temple in the Hollow, the mountaintop above Storm Ridge, the sea gate past the Tidepools' shelf, the caldera under the Ember Caves, the shrine in the Fen. Dev name: _the waylights_. They asked the oldest creature of each element to keep one lit. Those keepers are the raid bosses. They have kept the watch ever since: the temple grew over, the gate drowned, the shrine sank, and they stayed.

### S05. Why the bosses come

The stayers' children moved away down the four roads, a few at a time, and the tending stopped. The waylights burn low. The keepers are not attacking the valley. When new fires appear in it (the bonfire, the Forge), each keeper in turn comes toward the only light it can see, to fetch fire for its lamp. A mountain of sea water walking at your home looks like a threat, so the valley goes out to meet it. It goes back without what it came for, and comes again with "a new trick and a long memory". Nobody in the valley knows this. The bosses are never villains, and the fights are never wrong to fight: the keepers are also finding out whether the new folk can hold a watch.

### S06. The Festival

The Festival is the stayers' night of lighting every lantern at once, so the valley showed from the dark. It is the one part of the promise that never lapsed, because it turned into a party. "Everyone remembers why they came" is true of nobody. Hearthshell carries a coal of the promise fire, which is why its fire never goes out. Lanternfin spends the time between Festivals out in the dark water with its lantern, looking.

### S07. The clerk

One stayer kept the valley's records to the end: the neat, dry hand of the blueprints and the Lost Pages. Dev name: _the clerk_. The clerk writes about practical things and mentions the rest in passing, as things everyone knows ("the usual five", "when they are back"). The clerk never explains, and stopped writing mid-ledger. The clerk is the nearest thing the story has to a narrator, and is met only as handwriting.

### S08. The fifth arm

The Crossroads signpost had five arms. The fifth pointed to the Verge. Someone who could not bear to look at it any longer took it down and gave it to the bog, which keeps what it is given. The four roads are the ways the stayers' children left by. The "fifth shadow" at dusk is where the arm was.

### S09. The roll

The door "older than any road" is the stayers' lamp room. Inside are the roll (the names of everyone who walked out) and one unlit lantern for each name, to be lit when its owner is home. The old symbol on the lid of the lost caches is a ring with a dot in it: a lamp seen from above. The caches are the walkers' own things, packed and left for their return.

### S10. The cellars and the sunken door

The daily dungeon is the first valley's cellars and store rooms. The low night seeps up into them, so the rooms forget their order: that is why the map is new every day. The sunken door at the bottom opens onto the underside of the Verge. The Drowned Warden was posted there by the stayers so that nobody else would walk out. It opens every other gate for you instead.

### S11. The explorers

The players' explorers are the stayers' children's children, come back up the four roads without knowing it is a return. The wild creatures are the descendants of the first valley's creatures, which is why they come so readily "to the ones who wait". The creatures remember more than the people do, and show it only in habits.

### S12. The visitor

The weekly visitor walked out with the others and came back alone, long ago, by a way he cannot find again. He does not say so, and may not know it. Things that pass through his hands come back changed, which is all his reroll is.

### S13. The Games

The stayers started the Games to keep spirits up while they waited. The hut is older than the Meadow Committee. "It has always been on" is the committee's whole account of it.

### S14. What comes home

Void creatures are the ones who drifted, coming back. Each carries a piece of the night with one warm star in it: the star is the valley's light as it looked from out there, the thing they steered by. They come when the valley is bright enough: when the server has built its places up, fed the hearth and relit what was left to burn low.

### S15. The walkers themselves (held open)

Not dead. Out there it has been one long night. Whether and how they come home is the far end of the story and is **Misa's to decide**. Do not write toward an answer; do not close it off either.

## What is deliberately not decided

## The story in one breath

The valley has been keeping a light on for someone, for so long that it forgot who. The server that builds the valley up again makes it bright enough to be seen from the dark, and someone starts to come home.
