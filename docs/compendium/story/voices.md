# Voices: who is speaking, and what they do not know

Status: **direction accepted by Misa 2026-11-18; he does not read this file** (see `README.md`). **Safe to read for any writing job:** this file holds no story facts. (The stock of repeating names is in `ledger.md`, which does.)

Every fragment players read is spoken by someone inside the world. Nobody inside the world knows the whole truth, so no fragment can tell it. Pick the voice first; what it cannot know is your limit.

## Rules for voices

- **Which voice for which thing:** a find or a collection piece is described by the field notes (what it looks like, nothing more); a keepsake or a piece of furniture by the makers; a quirk by the creatures.
- **One voice per fragment.** The field notes do not suddenly know history. The clerk does not get wistful.
- **Knowing voices under-explain; ignorant voices mis-explain.** The clerk writes "the usual five" and stops. The committee says the long benches are "for the crowds", and there are no crowds.
- **Two voices may disagree, and neither is corrected.** A maker says the ring-and-dot is a lucky knot. A plaque uses it as a count. Leave both.
- **The creatures never get a caption.** "Sits facing the far hills at dusk." Full stop. Not "as if waiting for someone".
- **No new voice** without a row here. In particular: no prophet, no ghost, no talking boss, no diary that tells the whole thing, no wise elder who finally explains.

## Loaded things: keep them out of texture

The story uses a few ordinary things as its clues. A texture line that happens to use one becomes a false clue, or spends a real one early. If you are writing texture, **do not use any of these**; if your content truly needs one (a lamp for a furniture set), make it plain: no count, no size, no wear, nothing odd about it.

- **Lamps, lanterns, wicks, lamp oil, beacons,** and anything kept lit or left burning. (Shipped lantern lines stay as they are.)
- **The number five,** "the usual" anything, and counts of things in fives.
- **Things made for a crowd:** long benches, rows of hooks or pegs, big kettles and pots, batch numbers, large sizes, spare places at a table.
- **Signposts, arms, arrows, roads that lead nowhere,** a shadow where a thing used to be.
- **Facing, leaning or turning toward** a fire, the far hills or the edge of the valley; sitting and watching at dusk.
- **Ledgers, tallies, labels, lists of names,** handwriting.
- **Doors, gates and cellars** that are shut, sunk or unopened.
- **A ring with a dot in it,** or any maker's mark described in detail.
- **Creatures that know their way** round a place they have never been.
- **Leaving, returning, waiting for someone, keeping something for later.**

Plenty is left: weather, food, sleep, play, pride, clumsiness, friendship, the elements, how fights work.
